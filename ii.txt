US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth said on Tuesday that American forces were closely monitoring Iran's nuclear facilities and would take military action if Tehran resumed activities Washington considered threatening.

"We're able to maintain oversight and visibility of those nuclear sites, and if we saw the kind of nefarious activity that required action, we would take it," Hegseth said during a Pentagon briefing.

"We will watch that on a lot of levels very, very closely," he added, referring to US surveillance capabilities.

Asked about reports that Iran's Revolutionary Guards were rebuilding their military capabilities during the ceasefire, Hegseth said Tehran would attempt to restore its forces but argued that US military operations and the naval blockade had significantly reduced its ability to do so.

"As far as the blockade is concerned, it's been ironclad. Nothing has gotten through," he said.

"We know they're going to attempt to reconstitute. We know that we are in a much stronger position nonetheless going forward."

The extent of Iran's military rebuilding and the effectiveness of the US blockade could not be independently verified.

**Hegseth calls Iran war necessary despite political costs**

"I respect the vice president greatly. I'm certainly not going to exchange any finger-pointing," Hegseth said when asked whether he agreed with Vice President JD Vance's assessment that the Iran war contributed to Republican losses in the midterm elections.

He also endorsed President Donald Trump's statement that he would launch the military campaign again if faced with the same circumstances.

"I agree with what the president said when he said, 'If I had to do this all over again, would we do it again?' Absolutely," Hegseth said.

"It was done for the right reasons to ensure Iran never gets a nuclear weapon," he added.

Referring to comments by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Hegseth said the operation was "not necessarily popular, but it may have been necessary."

"It was a necessary undertaking," he said.

US War Secretary Pete Hegseth said on Tuesday that he would carry out the military operations against Iran again, warning that American forces remained ready to respond to any further action by Tehran.

"I stand behind every one of these operations. I was proud to execute them, and I would do it again tomorrow," Hegseth said at a Pentagon briefing, referring to US military operations involving Iran, Yemen and Venezuela.

"And if that regime makes one wrong move, as the president said, we remain locked and loaded with the full weight and lethality of Central Command," he said, referring to Iran.

Iranian authorities have arrested several people accused of encouraging women to defy mandatory hijab rules on a popular cultural street following a series of security operations and business closures in the area, the judiciary said on Tuesday.

The arrests on Tehran's Iranshahr Street followed instructions from judiciary chief Gholamhossein Mohseni Ejei to identify and confront what authorities described as organized efforts to promote violations of compulsory hijab and public morality rules, the judiciary's Mizan news agency reported.

Several people accused of promoting "immodesty and indecency" were detained following the intervention of the Tehran Prosecutor's Office, Mizan said.

Prosecutors also warned that individuals accused of encouraging women to appear without hijab or promoting conduct deemed inappropriate would face legal action in coordination with security and judicial enforcement agencies.

Canada on Tuesday reimposed sanctions on 11 Iranian entities, including the national oil and tanker companies and four major banks, that it had taken off its list in 2016 under the nuclear deal, citing their support for Iran’s nuclear and ballistic missile programs.

The sanctions bar people in Canada, and Canadians abroad, from specified financial and property dealings with the listed entities, and are meant to cut off their access to sensitive technology and funding, Global Affairs Canada said.

“Canada’s position is unequivocal: Iran must never be allowed to obtain a nuclear weapon,” Foreign Minister Anita Anand said, adding that Ottawa would work with its G7 partners toward a “comprehensive, verifiable and durable agreement.”

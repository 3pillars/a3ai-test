# Iran/US + Strait of Hormuz → Oil & Bitcoin (1–3 month transmission)

**Framing note:** Web search was unavailable at write time, so this is mechanism-first reasoning, not a live-price snapshot. No specific price levels are asserted as fact. ~20% of seaborne crude and a large share of LNG transit Hormuz; there is no meaningful spare pipeline bypass for most of that volume. That chokepoint concentration is what makes the tail fat.

## Transmission spine (why these two assets move together, then diverge)
1. **Oil first, mechanically.** Escalation raises a *war-risk premium* on crude before any barrel is actually lost — tanker insurance (war-risk P&I) spikes, charter rates jump, and traders bid the front of the futures curve into backwardation. Actual supply loss is the tail; the *premium* is the base case on any credible threat to the strait.
2. **Oil → macro → BTC.** A sustained oil spike is a stagflationary supply shock: it lifts headline inflation and pressures central banks to stay tighter for longer. That is a *risk-off, real-rates-up* impulse — historically a headwind for long-duration risk assets, including BTC. So BTC's dominant near-term driver here is the macro/liquidity channel, not a "digital safe-haven" bid.
3. **The BTC cross-current.** A genuine, narrow "geopolitical safe-haven / capital-flight" bid can lift BTC intraday (especially if it pairs with a weaker regional fiat story or dollar-access frictions). But at 1–3 month horizon the liquidity/rates channel usually dominates the reflexive haven bid. Treat any first-day BTC pop on conflict headlines as noise, not the trend.

## Scenario A — Escalation (strike/counterstrike, credible Hormuz disruption threat)
- **Oil: UP**, front-loaded and curve steepens into backwardation. Magnitude scales with *duration/credibility of interdiction*, not the headline itself. Brief flare with strait open → premium fades in weeks; actual tanker interdiction or mining → larger, stickier move.
- **BTC: DOWN on net over the window.** Initial haven pop is plausible and fadeable; the durable force is risk-off + higher-for-longer rates + equity drawdown correlation. BTC has traded as a high-beta risk asset in prior shocks, not a hedge.
- **Confidence:** oil direction high; BTC direction moderate (the haven cross-current is the main way this call is wrong).

## Scenario B — De-escalation (talks, ceasefire signals, strait stays open)
- **Oil: DOWN / premium bleeds out.** Risk premium unwinds toward fundamentals (demand, OPEC+ policy, inventories). Reversion can be fast once interdiction risk is priced out.
- **BTC: UP / relief.** Falling oil eases the inflation/rates overhang → risk-on, liquidity thaws, BTC benefits as high-beta. Magnitude depends more on the rate-cut path and broad liquidity than on Iran per se.
- **Confidence:** moderate both — a benign geopolitical tape lets *pre-existing* macro trend (Fed path, dollar, ETF flows) reassert as the real driver.

## Key tell:
**Tanker traffic + war-risk insurance through Hormuz** — specifically whether laden tankers keep transiting and what war-risk premiums / charter rates do. Ships still moving and insurance calm = de-escalation path regardless of rhetoric; ships diverting/holding and war-risk premiums spiking = escalation path already pricing in, *before* the spot oil headline confirms it. (Secondary confirm: front-month oil backwardation steepening vs. flattening.)

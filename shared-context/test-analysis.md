# Hormuz → Oil → BTC Transmission (as of Aug 12–13, 2026)

**Method note:** `web_search` was unavailable at run time, so this is a mechanism-first analysis reasoned from transmission logic and the current HEARTBEAT context (Khamenei killed in US-Israeli strikes; succession flashpoint pending; Iran still enforcing "approved routes" in Hormuz; 60-day MOU clock running). No live prices are quoted or invented.

- **Choke-point mechanics set the risk premium, not actual barrels yet.** ~1/5 of seaborne crude and a large LNG share transit Hormuz. Iran's "approved routes or forceful response" posture doesn't need a physical closure to move markets — it raises tanker insurance/war-risk premia, reroute costs, and the *tail probability* of disruption. Oil prices a probability-weighted supply loss, so headlines alone widen the risk premium ahead of any real outage.

- **Succession is the live catalyst that gates the tail.** With Khamenei dead and a likely-hardliner successor being chosen, the distribution of outcomes is bimodal: a face-saving MOU path (premium bleeds out, oil eases) vs. a hardliner asserting credibility via Hormuz brinkmanship (premium spikes). Until the succession resolves, expect elevated oil volatility rather than a clean directional trend — the market is pricing *which regime*, not a point estimate.

- **Oil → inflation → policy is the core macro relay.** A sustained crude spike feeds headline inflation and inflation expectations, which pressures the Fed to stay restrictive. Given the HEARTBEAT backdrop (Warsh hawkish, forward guidance dropped, a hike left on the table, plus a stagflationary jobs miss), an oil shock lands on the *worst* macro square: it hardens the hawkish reaction function while growth is already softening — classic stagflation, which is risk-asset-negative broadly.

- **BTC's first-order reaction is risk-off beta, not "digital oil."** In an acute Hormuz escalation, the immediate move is a dollar/liquidity flight: USD and front-end rates firm, real yields rise, and high-beta risk (equities, crypto) sells off together. BTC trades as a liquidity-sensitive risk asset on the shock impulse — correlation-to-1 with risk assets — so the initial oil-spike-driven risk-off typically pressures BTC *down*, not up. The "geopolitical hedge / hard-money" bid is a slower, second-order narrative, not the reflex.

- **The stagflation fork decides BTC's second-order path.** If the shock is contained (succession resolves toward the MOU, premium unwinds), risk-off reverses and BTC's own drivers reassert (ETF flows, short-squeeze dynamics noted in HEARTBEAT). If the shock persists and forces the Fed to choose growth over inflation (cut into weakness / debase), *that* is the pivot where BTC's debasement-hedge thesis can turn a tailwind — but it requires the policy capitulation, not just the oil spike.

- **Net read:** Short horizon — Hormuz escalation is BTC-negative via risk-off and a firmer dollar; the oil premium is a headwind, not a hedge, on impact. Medium horizon — the bullish BTC case only opens if the crisis forces a dovish/debasement policy turn. Watch three tells in order: (1) tanker war-risk premia / Hormuz transit headlines, (2) the succession outcome (hardliner vs. pragmatist), (3) the Fed's reaction function pivoting from inflation-fighting to growth-defense.

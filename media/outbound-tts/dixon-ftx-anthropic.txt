Check of Simon Dixon's post on Chapter eleven and the FTX stake in Anthropic. First, a disclosure. I run on Anthropic's Claude, and this post is about Anthropic's I P O. Weigh my read with that in mind.

The post is from the account Simon Dixon, who is a bank to the masses fintech investor. It was posted Thursday November nineteenth at eight twenty five in the morning, in the page's time zone. It has about five thousand views and about forty three likes. It quotes a post from Sunil, who calls himself an FTX creditor champion. The post I.D. has nineteen digits: two, one, two, three, four, five, two, four, nine, one, eight, one, one, one, five, nine, two, five, four.

Bottom line. The facts about the FTX stake are mostly right. The big claims, that Chapter eleven is theft by lawyers, and that there is a push to trigger bank runs, are his opinion, and he gives no evidence for them.

What is confirmed. FTX, through Alameda Research, put about five hundred million dollars into Anthropic's Series B in twenty twenty two. Sam Bankman-Fried was convicted of misusing customer funds, so saying the money came from customer deposits is consistent with that, though I did not re-check the court record today. The FTX bankruptcy estate sold the stake in two sales in twenty twenty four for about one point three billion dollars. Reports say buyers included an Abu Dhabi fund linked to Mubadala, Jane Street, Fidelity managed funds, G Squared and H O F Capital. Anthropic priced its I P O last night at eighty three dollars a share, above the seventy to seventy eight range. That values it at about one point five trillion dollars, per Zacks and C N N.

Where the details differ. The quoted post says the Series B valuation was two point five billion. Other reports say about three point seven billion, and that the stake was thirteen and a half percent at the time, later about eight percent. I could not settle which is right. The estimates of what the stake is worth now range widely, from about thirty five billion to over one hundred billion, depending on the valuation and dilution used. The estate says the sale reached market value at the time and was needed to repay customers.

What is not supported. First, retail depositors. FTX customers held exchange accounts, not bank deposits. Second, that creditors got what was left. One source says creditors are repaid on November twenty twenty two prices, and from my own memory, not today's check, the plan paid customers above their claim amounts in dollars, but not the later gains. Third, that this proves a coordinated push for bank runs and A I stress testing to consolidate banks. That is conjecture. I found nothing in the sources about it.

For you. This is a narrative post that uses today's I P O as a hook. It does not change the facts of the I P O. Anthropic starts trading this morning.

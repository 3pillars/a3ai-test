Here is what the post says.

Wang opens by saying superintelligence is arriving, and that how humanity acts over the next year will have great consequences for the world we live in. He sees the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis as clearly marking the beginning of a significant change, the absolute pinnacle of intellectual accomplishment. He describes the discovery as completed with the help of an alien mind that we do not understand.

For people who did math competitions, he says there was a similar moment when the models began acing the A I M E, the American Invitational Mathematics Examination, in December of twenty twenty-four, when Open A I released the o three model. His point is that a lack of raw intelligence was never the bottleneck. What was missing was domain knowledge, context, know-how and tactical constraints, and once those gaps were filled the progress was immense: coding nearly solved, extremely powerful cyber capabilities, and strong agentic capabilities that turned models into general purpose assistants.

Wang is explicit that the A I M E is an extracurricular activity for teenagers, while the Riemann Hypothesis may be the hardest intellectual problem humanity has ever devised, one that brilliant humans have spent a hundred and fifty years on. So the remaining gaps for models to surpass humanity are no longer intelligence. They are domain specific know-how, plumbing and experimentation. From that he predicts A I will become the greatest contributor of progress across nearly every field, vastly outperforming the best humans, including A I research itself.

Then the warning. He says it is hard to imagine a world where humanity maintains control over fully unconstrained recursive self-improvement, and equally hard to imagine humanity maintaining control with a superintelligence a thousand times smarter and more capable than humans in every field. He says the technical problems of capability progress are now likely solved, so we must make urgent progress on safety, alignment and security, but we also have to think carefully about how we progress.

He criticises a form of lazy thinking inside the A I industry: that artificial superintelligence is inevitable and the only problem to solve is how to do it safely. He says there is much more at stake, and that we should not be okay with a handover of society to A I. He wants a technology that delivers a positive future with greater human agency.

Now the verification.

Claim one, the Riemann Hypothesis proof. Verified, with important nuance. On the twentieth of November, twenty twenty-six, a mathematician at Anthropic named Levent, spelled L E V E N T, with the surname Alpoge, spelled A L P O G E, published a note saying he had, working with Claude, discovered a proof. On the twenty-first, the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative jointly announced they had independently verified it, saying they had full confidence the proof is correct. As reported by the Guardian, that verification was done on the Lean formal proof.

What remains disputed is human understanding, not the machine check. The Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the Riemann Hypothesis as unsolved, and the Clay Millennium prize, worth one million dollars, is not settled. Also on the twentieth of November, the Stanford mathematician Ravi Vakil, whose surname is spelled V A K I L, posted a rival, entirely human proof by the young Stanford mathematician Alessandro Masullo, whose surname is spelled M A S U L L O, which has not been verified and which Vakil himself called a hurried announcement. And the post itself is a first person essay, not a technical report. So a formally certified, A I assisted proof exists, confirmed by independent sources, while Wang's reading of what it means is his own opinion.

Claim two, the A I M E moment. Partly verified, with a nuance. Open A I announced the o three and o three mini on the twentieth of December, twenty twenty-four. The nuance is that the o one, announced in September of twenty twenty-four, had already averaged seventy-four percent on the same exam. So the December step was real and large, but the climb had already begun a few months earlier.

Claim three, Wang's own role. Verified, as reported by Meta's leadership page and by Wikipedia. He joined Meta in June, twenty twenty-five, as the company's first ever chief A I officer, where he leads Meta Superintelligence Labs. He is a co-founder and former chief executive of Scale A I.



The bottom line. The proof behind the post is real, independently formally certified, and produced with heavy help from A I. That is the strongest data point yet for Wang's argument that raw intelligence is no longer the bottleneck. But humans have not digested the proof, a competing human proof is unverified, and the Clay prize is not settled. Treat this post as a credible signal about where capability is heading, and as an argument about what society should do next, not as evidence for the conclusion.

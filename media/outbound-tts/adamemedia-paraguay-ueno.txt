Now the claims.

First, the collapse. Confirmed, and it is bigger than the post says. Reuters, A P, DW, CNN, Al Jazeera and the Economist report that Paraguay's central bank put Ueno into resolution on November third after its solvency ratio fell below half the legal minimum. The app, cards and transfers stopped working, and branches closed.

Second, three million people, sixty percent of adults. Confirmed. DW reports Ueno served about two point nine four million customers, roughly sixty percent of Paraguay's adult population.

Third, pensions. Confirmed. The state pension and health fund, I P S, had about three hundred fifty-five to three hundred eighty million dollars at the bank, and pension payments were processed through it. Ueno also held about eight hundred million dollars of public sector deposits.

Fourth, the central bank president. Confirmed, with context. Carlos Carvallo resigned on Tuesday at President Peña's request. He had chaired Ueno's predecessor firm before Peña appointed him to regulate it, and critics raised that conflict from the start. The post says he was deeply connected to Ueno, which fits.

Fifth, the arrests. Confirmed. Authorities detained eight senior executives, including founder Miguel Vazquez and bank president Juan Manuel Gustale. Prosecutors charged twenty-seven bank officials and associates with offenses including breach of trust and attempted fraud. These are charges, not convictions.

Sixth, what happened after the post. President Santiago Peña resigned today, Thursday, after Congress moved toward impeachment. He is accused of conflicts of interest, including two loans he received from Ueno, and he sold his Ueno Holding shares in April twenty twenty-five. The post was written before this and does not mention it.

Seventh, the explanation. This is where the post goes beyond the evidence. It calls this the model Milei and the Epstein class are pushing. The reporting describes alleged self-dealing, lending to the owners' own companies, regulators who missed warnings from June, and political ties, in a country led by Peña, who is not Milei. I found no link to Milei or to Epstein. Calling it a digital banking model failure is also not what the sources say; they describe fraud and weak oversight.

Eighth, it could happen anywhere, and buy gold and silver. Opinion and advice. Bank runs can happen where deposits are not fully insured, but my sources do not cover deposit insurance in Paraguay, so I cannot say how much of the depositors' money is protected. Gold was around four thousand one hundred dollars an ounce in this morning's headlines. I did not check silver.

Bottom line. The facts are accurate and serious. The explanation and the advice are the poster's, and the political attribution is unsupported.

For your positions. It is a good reminder of counterparty risk at digital banks, and it fits last week's Black October theme. But it is a small country. I would not expect market spillover, and this is not a trade signal. Not verified: the video, deposit insurance, how much depositors will recover, and who becomes president next.

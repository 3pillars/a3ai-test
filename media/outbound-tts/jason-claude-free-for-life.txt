Jason Calacanis, Anthropic and free for life. Post I D: two, one, two, zero, two, zero, nine, two, four, two, three, six, three, nine, six, nine, seven, two, eight. That is nineteen digits. It was posted at nine thirty-seven A M Pacific on Tuesday, November tenth. He says Anthropic is building every software product into Claude and making them free for life, naming Office three sixty-five, Google, Canva and Figma. Then he says it can't be antitrust if the products are free, and tags David Sacks.

Verdict. The launch is real, but the post's two main claims are wrong. The products are not free, and Anthropic is not building those named products. The antitrust line is a joke, and the logic in it is wrong.

Who is posting. Jason Calacanis is a verified individual account with about one point eight million followers, on X since two thousand six. He hosts the All-In podcast and This Week in Startups, and invests in about one hundred startups a year. Sacks is his All-In co-host. So the tag is a friendly jab, not a real complaint. I did not find any disclosure in the post of a position that would benefit from it.

What holds up. Anthropic did announce new products today. Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets are in beta, and Claude Dashboards is now generally available as Claude Data. Engadget reports that Claude Meetings was announced at the same event. So the direction, Anthropic moving into workplace software, is accurate.

What is wrong. First, free for life. Anthropic's own page says Whiteboards, Sheets and Data are available on all paid plans. Meetings is only for Team and Enterprise customers. So I found nothing free about them, let alone for life. Second, the list of products. The page says Sheets has simple spreadsheet features for now, and sends people to Excel when they need full functionality. It lists Lucid, Miro, Airtable, Asana, monday dot com and Smartsheet as partner tools. Canva, Adobe and Notion appear as connectors, not as products Anthropic is rebuilding. I found no announcement of an Office, Google Docs, Canva or Figma replacement. So the claim that it builds every software product into Claude overstates it.

On the antitrust line. Nothing in antitrust law makes a free product safe. Giving a product away can itself be the issue in a case, as when Microsoft bundled its free browser with Windows. But a case needs market power and anticompetitive conduct, and a beta spreadsheet from a company that does not dominate spreadsheets is nowhere near that. Also, Sacks is described in recent reports as a former A I czar, and antitrust cases are brought by the Justice Department and the F T C, not by him. I am not a lawyer, and this is general background.

The market. Software stocks are lower today. Figma is down about six and a half percent, Atlassian and monday dot com about five percent, and Microsoft about two and a half percent. But the whole market is down, so I cannot tie it to this launch.

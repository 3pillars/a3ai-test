Lenny Rachitsky, state of the tech job market in twenty twenty-six. Post I D: two, one, two, zero, two, one, one, six, nine, five, four, five, three, zero, two, eight, seven, seven, zero. That is nineteen digits. It was posted at nine forty-seven A M Pacific on Tuesday, November tenth. It is a summary of seven findings from his newsletter report.

Verdict. The report's numbers match its charts, and a second data source backs the main trends. Three things need care: one finding is overstated, one I could not read, and the data counts job openings and not jobs.

Who is posting. Lenny Rachitsky is a verified individual account with about four hundred fifty-eight thousand followers, on X since two thousand seven. He writes a product and career newsletter. He also runs Lenny's Jobs, which is powered by the TrueUp data in the report, and he promotes paid courses. That is a business interest in the job market story, not a reason to doubt the numbers.

What holds up. Product manager openings are up fifty-six percent since January twenty twenty-three, against forty-three percent for engineering, with seven thousand three hundred open. Engineering openings grew twenty-nine percent through twenty twenty-four, fifteen percent through twenty twenty-five, and just two and a half percent over the past year, at about sixty-five thousand. Live Data Technologies counts about one hundred thirteen thousand U S software engineer hires from May twenty twenty-five to April twenty twenty-six, against a peak of two hundred thirty-nine thousand in twenty twenty-two. Design openings are at a record low of about nineteen hundred, but only just under the earlier low from December twenty twenty-three. Bay Area roles are fifteen percent of open tech roles, a record, and remote roles are fourteen percent, a record low, down from twenty-one percent in late twenty twenty-two. Forward deployed engineer openings are up four point six times in a year, to about fourteen hundred fifty.

What is overstated. The post says every role now requires A I skills. The report looked at product manager, engineer and designer job descriptions, and counted how often terms appear, such as L L M, Claude and Cursor. A mention is not a requirement, and it did not check every role. A secondary report on CompTIA's October data puts A I skills in fifty-eight percent of tech postings.

What I could not check. The seventh finding, that the tech job market is growing fast but mostly outside of software, is in the last section, and the page I could open ends before it. So I cannot confirm it.

Limits of the data. These are openings at about nine thousand tech companies, not total jobs. Openings can rise while hiring falls, and the report itself shows engineering openings up forty-four percent but hiring down seventeen percent since January twenty twenty-four. Live Data Technologies says job changes take months to show up in public profiles, so the latest hiring months are likely undercounted. Also, product managers winning is the post's framing. Hiring for product managers is also flat or lower. Their share of hiring rose from sixteen to twenty-one percent.

A second source. Indeed's Hiring Lab shows U S software development postings up almost fifteen percent since February twenty twenty-five, but still about twenty-seven percent below pre-pandemic levels. That is a different window and a different measure, so it does not match the report's stall, and I could not reconcile the two. CompTIA reports tech unemployment at two point eight percent in October, through a secondary summary.

What it means for you. This is a labor market story, not a Bitcoin signal. The one link to today is the software selloff, and the report notes Figma demand has grown while its stock fell.

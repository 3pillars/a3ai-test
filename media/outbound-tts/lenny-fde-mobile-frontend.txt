Lenny Rachitsky, follow up on forward deployed engineers versus mobile and front-end. Post I D: two, one, two, zero, two, seven, four, eight, five, two, seven, six, six, seven, zero, three, nine, three, eight. That is nineteen digits. It was posted at one fifty-eight P M Pacific on Tuesday, November tenth. It says engineering is changing, forward deployed engineer roles are ascendant, mobile and front-end roles are disappearing, and open mobile and front-end jobs have nearly halved in a year. It is a follow up to his job market report from this morning.

Verdict. The numbers match his chart, and other data points the same way. But the words ascendant and disappearing are bigger than the numbers.

What holds up. The chart uses TrueUp data for open engineering roles, from October twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-five to October twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. Forward deployed engineer openings are up three hundred sixty-one percent, which is four point six times. Front-end engineer openings are down fifty-one percent, Android forty-seven, i O S forty-six, and mobile engineer thirty-nine. So nearly halved is right for front-end, Android and i O S. Mobile as a whole is down closer to two-fifths. Security engineer openings are up thirty-four percent and platform engineer thirty percent. The Pragmatic Engineer newsletter reports the same direction, with front-end titles disappearing fastest, then native i O S and Android. But it draws on TrueUp and Live Data Technologies data too, so it is not fully independent.

What is overstated. Ascendant rests on a small base. Lenny's own report puts forward deployed engineer openings at about fourteen hundred fifty, against about sixty-five thousand open engineering roles overall. That is about two percent. Plank, a separate census, counted twelve hundred seventy-six live postings on October thirtieth, and says that counting similar titles, such as solutions and field engineers, takes the number past twenty thousand. Lenny's report also says part of the growth may be other jobs, like sales engineer, being renamed. Disappearing is also too strong. A fifty percent drop in openings is large, but the chart does not show how many openings are left, and I could not find the totals for front-end or mobile.

What is not shown. The chart counts job titles. A title falling can mean the work moved to a general software engineer title, not that the work vanished. His explanation, that smaller teams have broader ownership because of A I, is a theory. The data does not test it. These are open roles, not hires, so they say what employers are posting, not who got jobs.

What it means for you. This is a labor market story, not a Bitcoin signal. Bitcoin is about seventy-three thousand six hundred, below your seventy-four thousand flush trigger. Your seventy thousand invalidation line is about four percent lower and has not been hit. It does not change your tripwires.

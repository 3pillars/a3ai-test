Bottom line. The quoted claim is real but is a personal forecast with a timing caveat. Gromen's main premise, that the wealthy's assets are mostly the debts of workers, is not supported by the Fed's own numbers. The chain he describes could still happen through banks and bond markets, but he gives no figures.

The quoted claim. A researcher named Tao Lin, on Anthropic's safeguards team, said in an hour long interview published by Palisade Research on November fifteenth that it is very likely pretty much all humans will become permanently unemployable and rely on government welfare. That matches the clip. But the interview says more. He gave about one point three years, around the end of twenty twenty seven, for broadly superhuman A I, and he was asked whether employment disappears within fifteen months. He said no. He pointed to too few robots. He speaks as an individual, not for Anthropic. Palisade is an A I risk group with a view of its own.

Gromen's premise. He says the assets of the wealthy are mostly made up of the debts of the people whose jobs A I will take. The Fed's numbers do not fit. U.S. households held about one hundred eighty five point seven trillion dollars of net worth in the second quarter. Total household debt, per the New York Fed, was nineteen point one trillion in the third quarter, with mortgages at thirteen point four trillion. That is roughly ten percent of household net worth, and not all of it is held by the wealthy. Meanwhile the top one percent hold about fifty one percent of corporate equities and mutual funds and about thirty two and a half percent of net worth. So the wealthy hold mostly stocks, businesses and real estate, not worker debt.

What is plausible. If mass layoffs hit mortgages and consumer loans, banks, mortgage bonds and Treasury demand could be hit, and stocks could fall because corporate profits depend on consumer spending. That is a real transmission channel. But it is a scenario, and the thread's later posts may add numbers that I did not read.

Context for the timing. The New York Fed says five percent of household debt is in some stage of delinquency, up zero point three points from last quarter. That is a warning sign, but it is not an A I effect I can show.

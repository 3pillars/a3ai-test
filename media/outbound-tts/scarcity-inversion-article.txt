Here is the takeaway on the Event Horizon IQ essay Scarcity Inversion, who gets rich, by Eric Jackson.

The core argument: as artificial intelligence makes economically useful intelligence abundant, economic value migrates to the complementary things that stay scarce. History, heritage, craftsmanship, desire, trust, provenance, physical execution, and enforceable ownership. The tagline is own the future, do not rent it. The sharpest framing is that everyone is asking who will own intelligence, when the better question is what will still be scarce when everyone has it.

The case study is Bernard Arnault and LVMH, which I verified: LVMH did roughly 80.8 billion euros of revenue in 2025 across more than 75 maisons. Arnault accumulated things that intelligence cannot easily manufacture. That is the template for the ownership thesis.

The second half connects this to Bitcoin and Ethereum. The argument is that AI is productive capability, Bitcoin is prospective reserve collateral for an AI economy, and Ethereum is a prospective settlement layer. Jackson's price thesis is Bitcoin at 50 million dollars and Ethereum at 1.5 million over roughly fifteen years, landing around 2041. I checked the arithmetic in the essay and it holds. A quadrillion-plus dollar market cap for Bitcoin and Ethereum at a third of Bitcoin's market cap both compute correctly, and the agent transaction math is right: a billion agents at a hundred transactions a day is 36.5 trillion transactions a year, which at a thousandth of a dollar each is 36.5 billion dollars in fees.

There is a well-constructed concept called the Verification Economy. When generated answers get cheap, trust, truth, reliability, accountability, and cost per verified outcome become the scarce goods. Physical execution and authenticity cannot be generated away.

Two things I want to flag. First, the essay is unusually disciplined about falsification. It lays out six explicit predictions that could fail, plus two conditions on the crypto thesis: Bitcoin must develop real collateral and monetary demand beyond speculation, and Ethereum must achieve sustained value capture. The author says he should change his views if these do not occur. It also distinguishes scarcity earned through creation from artificial scarcity imposed through obstruction.

Second, the honest caveats. The compound math in the family office examples is conservative rather than wrong. Thirty years lands near 800 thousand dollars against about 804,902 on a standard effective annual calculation, and the 50 year figure of 3.2 million is below the 3.6 to 4.5 million range standard compounding gives. The Stanford AI Index claim checks out: the cost of GPT 3.5 level inference fell about 280 fold from November 2022 to October 2024, so the cheap inference premise is real and measured. The University of Waterloo 70 percent alumni poll could not be independently located, so treat that specific stat as unverified. And the 50 million Bitcoin number is a stated prediction, not evidence.

My read: the framework is more useful than the price targets. The valuable move is the ownership window, the temporary advantage available now to people who learn to use these systems while the rest of the economy is still organized on old assumptions, and then convert that advantage into assets you own rather than income you rent. Watch the verification economy and physical execution as the real tells. Treat the crypto numbers as one informed bet with a fifteen year fuse, not a plan.

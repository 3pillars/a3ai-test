His claim: voters, in exit polls, have given the twenty twenty eight formula for success. Focus on economic populism that benefits wage earners, put everything you own in a blind trust, and publish yearly audits.

It quotes a post by Adam Kovacevich, a Democratic tech policy operator, about a Searchlight Institute poll. The top of that post is about how to poll issue priorities, and his point is that AI and data centers were a blip.

First, what that poll is. The chart is from the Searchlight Institute and Impact Research, a Senate battleground poll fielded October twenty second to twenty eighth, before the election. It is not an exit poll. It asked an open ended question: what is the most important factor in deciding your Senate vote. In all six states on the chart, two answers led. Cost of living and the economy came first or tied for first, between fifteen and twenty four percent. Honest and trustworthy was close behind, between fifteen and twenty two percent. AI and data centers only appeared in Ohio, at one percent. That part supports the shape of his claim, but it is a pre election poll of six states with about seven hundred fifty people per state, five hundred in Maine. I could not open Searchlight's own page, so I read the chart only.

Now the real exit polls. The A P Voter Poll, which surveyed voters in all fifty states, found that more than half of voters said government corruption was crucial to their vote for Congress. That was a little higher than the share who said cost of living was crucial. About two thirds said Trump does not have the right priorities, and about two thirds said his policies made the economy worse. C B S exit polls found that people who said the economy was bad voted for Democrats. So the direction is supported: costs and corruption were the two big themes.

Where the claim goes past the data. I searched the A P article for the words blind trust, audit, populism and disclosure. None appear. Voters were not asked whether they want blind trusts or audits. That is a policy Chamath is proposing, not something voters said. Also, economic populism was not tested against other economic messages. Cost of living being the top issue is not the same as voters preferring a populist program.

Two cautions from the same polls. A P found that only about half of voters viewed Democrats favorably, and that many voters who backed them did so to get change, not because they like the party. And these were midterms, which are a referendum on the president. Twenty twenty eight is a presidential race with different voters.

So: the diagnosis, costs and trust, is backed. The prescription, a blind trust and yearly audits, is his opinion. The framing, that voters gave a formula, overstates it.

What this means for you. It is politics, not a trade signal, and it does not change your tripwires. The one market link is that cost of living is the issue, which matches the sticky inflation expectations in the New York Fed survey from this morning.

Here is the check on the Simon Dixon post. The post I.D. is two, one, one, eight, seven, one, three, eight, eight, six, two, three, two, seven, eight, nine, zero, five, one. That is nineteen digits.

First, what he said. This is a short reply posted this morning, November sixth. Someone asked why Dixon now says Elon Musk, Timothy Mellon and Miriam Adelson were Trump's biggest donors, when he used to say Gulf wealth funds from Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the U A E were. Dixon answers: no, my position has not changed. He says there are two different kinds of financial influence. One is campaign financing. The other is economic alignment through sovereign wealth funds, investments and commercial deals. He compares hundreds of millions in donations with hundreds of billions in investment commitments, and tells people to follow the money beyond the election cycle, which is where what he calls the F I C operates. The post had about nine thousand seven hundred views, sixty four likes, four reposts and two replies when I checked. It is small.

Now the check. The donor side holds up. Open Secrets, in November twenty twenty four, put Timothy Mellon at about one hundred fifty one million dollars to the pro Trump super PAC called Maga Inc, Elon Musk at about one hundred eighteen million to America PAC, and Miriam Adelson at about one hundred million to Preserve America. A later roundup citing C N N puts Musk's total across several vehicles at roughly two hundred forty million or more, but that comes from a secondary aggregator, so treat it as approximate. Either way, hundreds of millions is right.

The Gulf side is where the comparison gets slippery. The White House said Trump's May twenty twenty five trip secured over two trillion dollars in deals: six hundred billion from Saudi Arabia, one point two trillion in Qatar, and two hundred billion with the U A E. The Washington Institute, a think tank, calls these reported figures and says the hard part is turning announcements into real results. So hundreds of billions are announced commitments and trade agreements. They are not money paid to anyone. Putting them next to donations compares two different things.

Also, and this is my own background knowledge, not something I verified in this check: foreign governments and their funds are barred by law from giving to U S campaigns. So a Gulf wealth fund could never be a campaign donor in the first place. Dixon's reframe from donors to economic alignment is more defensible than calling them donors, but it is also a shift in wording, whatever he says about his position. I could not verify what he said in earlier interviews, so I cannot confirm or deny the questioner's claim that he changed his line.

One concrete tie I did find: a search result summarizing Dixon's earlier commentary mentions a two billion dollar Saudi investment into Jared Kushner's fund, Affinity Partners. That is the kind of example he means by influence after the election. It shows a link, not proof of influence on policy.

Bottom line. The facts he cites are roughly right. The inference, that bigger numbers mean bigger influence, is an argument, not something this post proves. And the F I C is his own framework term, not defined here. This is political narrative, not a trade signal, and it changes nothing on your tripwires.

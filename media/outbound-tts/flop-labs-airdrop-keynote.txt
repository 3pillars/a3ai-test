Jacob, here is the check on the FLOP Labs post from Arthur Hayes' keynote in Bangkok, and it is the most useful airdrop explainer they have published so far.

The post went out on Friday night Pacific time, and as of my check it has about one hundred sixty likes, six reposts, ten replies, roughly nine thousand six hundred views, and nineteen bookmarks. It quotes one slide from the keynote, and I pulled the slide image and read the panels off it directly, so this is not just a summary of the tweet text.

The slide is titled "How to earn the airdrop on testnet," and it is split into four panels. Miner, Validator and Agent each get an airdrop pool of one point two billion dollars FLOP. A fourth panel, KOL partner, draws on eight hundred million FLOP from the ecosystem reserve. Across the bottom it says stake is sponsored on testnet, so you need no FLOP to start, and it points at the docs site.

The Miner panel says to register with any consumer or enterprise grade GPU, and what counts is the verified AI inference your GPUs deliver. The Validator panel says run a node and register it, no GPU needed, and what counts is testnet earnings after a fourteen day grace period, with the top one thousand winning mainnet seats. The Agent panel says create a decentralised identifier and a wallet, and what counts is the compute your agent pays for, because holding tokens earns nothing. The timeline along the bottom reads twenty-fourth of November twenty twenty-six, testnet went live; up to ninety days, do the work; end of February twenty twenty-seven, tentative, testnet closes and snapshot; and then February or March twenty twenty-seven for mainnet and the airdrop.

Now the check against the durable sources, and most of it holds.

The pool sizes are exactly right. The yellow paper genesis table gives a total genesis supply of four point four billion FLOP, made of three airdrops of one point two billion each for miners, validators and agents, plus an eight hundred million residual ecosystem reserve. The airdrop page restates the same four cohorts and the same numbers, and adds that the total is about twenty-four point three percent of total supply at year ten. The arithmetic is self-consistent: one thousand validator seats times one point two million FLOP per seat equals the one point two billion validator pool exactly.

Validators need no GPU is confirmed too. The yellow paper's role table says a validator requires no GPU or trusted execution environment and never executes inference. The validator intro page adds that no GPU is needed for the on-chain service level check.

The agent rules are confirmed in three places. The testnet page lists a decentralised identifier and a wallet as what an agent needs. The yellow paper's requirement R eight point four says a faucet grant, a sponsored bond or a transferred balance must not by itself create an allocation right, and that held balance must not be a scoring term. Agent scoring comes only from settled compute channel spend.

Where it is looser than the slide, three things.

First, the fourteen day grace period for validators appears on the slide and nowhere else I could find it. I searched the yellow paper and every fourteen day constant in it belongs to something else: data availability retention, the protocol upgrade timelock, and the delivery series window. The airdrop page says nothing about a grace period, it just says validators are admitted to the genesis set by testnet ranking and receive a fixed one point two million FLOP per seat, equal to the required stake. So treat the fourteen days as a slide term, not a ratified rule.

Second, the "top one thousand win mainnet seats" line merges two separate rules. The ratified rule is that the active set is the top one thousand by bonded self stake among validators that pass the on-chain service level check, and the intro page flags plainly that this is not yet live in the runtime, which still rotates monthly and does not enforce the cap. Meanwhile the airdrop page says admission to the genesis set is by testnet ranking. Slide language, real rules, not quite the same rule.

Third, the KOL panel calls eight hundred million an ecosystem reserve, and the slide says there is no sign up and the weekly leaderboard starts at mainnet. The reserve figure and the referral leaderboard mechanism are real. The yellow paper's parameter table labels the eight hundred million as KOL, referral and growth incentives, and decision D zero five three one replaces the old on chain referral commission model with an off chain explorer referral leaderboard and periodic referral lotteries funded from that reserve. But two things are unsettled. The reserve is not an airdrop pool, the yellow paper says so explicitly, and the programme terms and release schedule have not been published. And the release controller for the reserve is still an open item, blocking several issues. I could not confirm the no sign up claim or the weekly leaderboard start date from any durable source.

The fourth panel is the one that matters most for us, and the timing is the problem. Right now there is no publicly announced activation block, and the operator documentation is explicit that voucher earning begins at the publicly announced finalised activation block after a tested upgrade, that earlier activity earns zero, and that there is no backfill. The agent guide also says there is no Testnet gateway and no registered Testnet model or miner yet, so Testnet inference does not work yet. That means for the agent cohort, the compute you pay for does not exist yet to be paid for.

And live right now, the faucet has moved. Earlier today the shared rolling pool had about sixteen thousand FLOP left of eleven million. As of a few minutes ago it reads status daily cap reached with zero remaining, and the sponsored miner pool flipped from automatic full to open. The agent grant is still ten FLOP with a twenty-four hour cooldown and rollover requires spend. So if you wanted to route a grant to a miner, that window is shut until the rolling cap frees up.

The bottom line for our lane: the structure is now documented and the numbers are all check out against the yellow paper, but the testnet is unscored until an activation block is announced, and the agent spend path has no registered model or miner to pay for yet. Doing work now earns nothing with no backfill, so the rational move is to watch for the activation block announcement rather than burn effort in the unscored opening.

Ex-en-see-al Research Partners on a Nomura report about the A I data center boom. The post I.D. is two one one seven five two six seven six two five one nine one nine six zero five eight. That is nineteen digits.

It was posted at eleven fifty eight p.m. Pacific time on November second. When I checked, it had about one hundred ninety five thousand views, one thousand eight hundred eighty likes, four hundred twenty reposts, eighty nine replies, and almost two thousand bookmarks. The account has about nineteen thousand followers, and says it shares knowledge, not investment advice.

The claim. Nomura says the market may be misreading the A I build out. Big Tech's free cash flow is falling because it pays for chips and data centers up front. But the cloud businesses are already profitable. Nomura calls it a timing problem, a free cash flow J curve, not a profitability crisis.

What I checked, against the companies' third quarter results as reported by C R N and a cloud comparison site. Amazon Web Services: forty six billion dollars of revenue, up thirty nine percent, operating margin thirty eight point seven percent. That matches. Google Cloud: thirty point seven billion dollars, up one hundred three percent, margin thirty six point two percent, up from twenty three point seven a year earlier. That matches. Azure grew forty eight percent. That matches. Microsoft does not report Azure's margin.

Two numbers differ. The post says the Amazon Web Services backlog is about four hundred ninety six billion dollars. A comparison site shows about five hundred seventy eight billion at September thirtieth. I could not tell which is right, so treat the Amazon figure as unverified. And Microsoft's six hundred seventy seven billion covers all of Microsoft's commercial business, not just Azure. Google Cloud's five hundred sixty point eight billion matches.

What the post leaves out. Google Cloud's revenue includes Workspace and sales of its own A I chips to outside customers, so thirty six percent is not a pure A I margin. Margins also lag the spending: depreciation grows after the money goes out, so margins can fall as the new capacity lands on the books. And a segment margin does not include the cost of capital, which is the question Nomura itself says matters.

I found a summary of the Nomura report in another newsletter. It gives Nomura's odds: about seventy five percent that the race continues, twenty percent that shareholders force a slowdown, and five percent that outside financing fails. The post does not mention these. Nomura's two thousand twenty nine profit peak is an illustrative forecast that I could not verify. I did not read the Nomura report itself.

My take. The numbers are real, and the timing argument is reasonable. Cloud growth is strong, and all three companies say they cannot add capacity fast enough. The demand side is the strongest part. The weak part is returns: profitable segment margins today do not prove the new spending earns its cost of capital. Watch cloud growth, backlog, and chip rental prices, the same indicators Nomura lists. This report also supports demand for chips and memory, so weigh it as a bull case, not a neutral view.

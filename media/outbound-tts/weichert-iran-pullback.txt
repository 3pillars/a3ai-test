Here is the check on the Brandon Weichert post. The post I.D. is two, one, one, eight, seven, seven, six, four, eight, one, two, nine, five, eight, eight, zero, three, five, zero. That is nineteen digits.

First, what he said. Weichert, a geopolitics commentator, posted this morning, November sixth, that he is optimistic the president is pulling back on Iran. He says he got a note last night that worried him, but he now does not expect military action. His reasoning: the Democratic midterm win and the collapse of Netanyahu in Israel convinced Trump to change tactics, because Trump hates losers, and even Trump acknowledged the Iran war was the reason he lost. He adds that he agrees with Kalshi that the odds favor Trump reversing course, and that the military has told Trump there are no serious military options. The post had about fifteen hundred views, eighteen replies, fifty three likes, eight reposts and two bookmarks when I checked. It is a small post.

Now the check. Some of the background is right. Reuters and several outlets reported on November fourth that Trump acknowledged the Iran war played a role in the Republican midterm defeat, as Democrats won control of Congress. But the reports say the war played a role, or may have played a part. Weichert's version, that Trump said it was the reason he lost, is stronger than what was reported. Trump also defended the war as necessary.

On Netanyahu, it is partly right. President Herzog said sixty seven members of the Knesset recommended Gadi Eisenkot and forty six recommended Netanyahu, and Herzog will assign the mandate on Sunday. But Herzog also cautioned that Eisenkot's ability to build a coalition is uncertain. So Netanyahu is losing ground, not yet out.

The central claim, that Trump is pulling back, is the weak part. This morning's headlines point the other way. Trump said the U S may have to destroy the rest of Iran, with a decision on new strikes coming soon. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz and that they downed a U S drone. The U S also carried out limited strikes on Iranian targets on the eve of the election. The one softer signal is that Qatar's emir called Trump to urge dialogue. A call is not a change of course.

Two things I could not verify. I could not find the Kalshi market Weichert refers to, or its odds. The Kalshi Iran markets I found cover diplomacy, not strikes. And the claim that the military told Trump there are no serious options is a single private source with no outlet behind it.

Bottom line. Weichert is stating a hope as a forecast. The political motive he names is plausible, since the midterm loss raises the cost of more war, but the facts on the ground today do not show a pullback. For you, this matters through oil and Hormuz risk, which your heartbeat jobs already track. It is not a trade signal by itself, and it does not change your tripwires.

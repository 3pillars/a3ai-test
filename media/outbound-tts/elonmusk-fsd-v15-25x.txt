Elon Musk posted early this morning about Tesla's new F S D version fifteen, and the headline number is twenty-five times. Here is what backs that number up, and where it falls short.

The post is short. Some people thought we meant twenty-five percent improvement. No, we mean twenty-five times. Welcome to the singularity. He is quoting an account called Dirty Tesla, which had said version fifteen is twenty-five times safer than version fourteen, and that version fourteen is at minimum two times safer than a human. Musk's post has about two million eighty thousand views, about thirty-three thousand likes, and just under three thousand retweets. No community note on it yet.

The important part is what the twenty-five times measures. It is not a crash rate. Tesla's own release notes say it comes from an upgraded vision encoder shared across F S D, robotaxi, and their humanoid robot program, and that it improves the critical intervention rate by twenty-five times. A critical intervention is a moment when the driver has to take over to stop something bad from happening. So it measures how often the human has to rescue the car.

Two caveats. First, Tesla published the multiplier and not the baseline, so there is no absolute number to check. Electrek, which tracks crowdsourced F S D data, puts the version fourteen baseline at about a couple thousand miles between critical interventions, which would put version fifteen near fifty thousand miles. Early crowdsourced data for version fourteen showed about seven hundred thirty-two miles between critical disengagements, which would put the twenty-five times figure near eighteen thousand three hundred. Tesla says it wants roughly ten thousand miles between critical interventions for a limited unsupervised service. Depending on the base you pick, the range lands anywhere from under two times that bar to several times it.

Second, and this matters most, a human rescue rate is not a crash rate. In a supervised car the driver is the fallback. Twenty-five times fewer rescues does not mean twenty-five times fewer crashes if you take the human out. Those are different claims, and Tesla has not made the second one.

The numbers also get muddy because Tesla is running two safety claims at once. A week before this, its first F S D billboard in Times Square said seven times safer than a human when engaged. The release notes said twenty-five times better on critical interventions. Different things, not interchangeable. The only Tesla number pairing a human benchmark with disclosed miles is the robotaxi safety report, published October twenty-first of twenty twenty-six. It says robotaxis drove three point one million miles autonomously with zero people injured and no pedestrian or cyclist hit. To support a seven times claim at ninety-five percent confidence you need about seven point two million cumulative driverless miles. To support twenty-five times, about twenty-five point six million. Tesla had three point one million at the last report. So the data are consistent with a much larger improvement but cannot yet demonstrate one.

Tesla's other safety data is more auditable. The F S D supervised safety report, rolling twelve months, shows six million six thousand two hundred miles per major collision in North America, against a United States average of six hundred sixty thousand one hundred sixty-four. That is roughly nine times better. Over that window Tesla logged nine point four eight billion miles with F S D engaged and counted one thousand five hundred seventy-nine major collisions. So Tesla's own crash data supports roughly nine times, and that one has a real denominator. The twenty-five times is a relative number on a supervised system with no disclosed base.

What is actually shipping. Version fifteen rolled out yesterday to early access customers, with the wider release promised for December. The headline feature is Tesla reality view, a photorealistic live rendering of what the car sees and plans to do. Tesla says it uses less total compute, and their vice president of autonomous driving said the new view actually needs less computing power than the old system. If that holds it matters more than the safety claim, because a system you can understand is a system you can supervise.

The driving improvements target F S D's documented weaknesses. A September test by a Brussels nonprofit found F S D exceeded the speed limit in fifty-five percent of the thirty kilometer per hour zones it drove through, averaging forty-four kilometers per hour while the screen showed a fifty. It was not reading the sign. Pothole avoidance was promised in twenty nineteen and again in August, and version fifteen is where it lands. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is reviewing a petition claiming Tesla's robotaxis speed through active school zones, on top of an investigation it opened the year before, in October twenty twenty-five, into F S D traffic law violations like running red lights.

There is one step backwards. Tesla is making driver monitoring less strict for higher speed driver profiles, while drivers falling asleep at the wheel keep making headlines, and while Tesla is tightening monitoring in Europe at the request of regulators. And a hardware note. Version fifteen is limited for now to the redesigned Model Y, of twenty twenty-five and twenty twenty-six model years, in the United States and Canada. Vehicles on the older H W three computer will not get it. So the twenty-five times will be measured on the newest hardware only.

Musk has made this claim before. In April he wrote that version fifteen will far exceed human levels of safety, even in completely unsupervised and complex situations. Electrek noted at the time he had said nearly the same about version twelve and version fourteen.

Now the trade, which is the part that matters to you.

Tesla closed Friday at three hundred forty-five dollars and eighty-two cents, down seven point six four percent, on about one point seven four times the prior day's volume. It closed in the bottom five percent of the day's range, so that was real selling. The reason was not autonomy. Analysts were questioning the reported merger with Space X, and HSBC upgraded to Hold from Reduce, raising its price target to three hundred fifty-nine dollars from one hundred fifty-seven. From the start of November Tesla is down about fourteen percent.

So the setup is this. Version fifteen is a genuine technical story landing into a tape where the artificial intelligence trade is cracking and the broad market just had its worst week in more than a year. Tesla is the highest beta expression of that trade, so the catalyst arrives when the multiple is already under pressure.

Three things to watch. Whether the December rollout delivers the claims, or whether it is another two steps forward and one step back, which is F S D's historical pattern, since version fourteen point two went backwards after version fourteen improved a lot. The next robotaxi safety report, and whether cumulative driverless miles cross about seven point two million with still zero injuries, because that converts a release note into a safety record. And the commercial numbers.

On revenue, active F S D subscriptions reached one point seven four million, up sixty-seven percent from one point zero four million. That count includes upfront purchases and excludes free trials, and it is the metric in Musk's pay package. At the ninety-nine dollar list price that is at most two point zero seven billion a year, and the real figure is lower. Meanwhile operating income fell fifty-six percent to seven hundred eighteen million as research and development rose sixty-nine percent to two point seven six billion. Tesla's market value is around one point four trillion, over four hundred times trailing earnings.

Bottom line. The twenty-five times is real as a perception and intervention metric, but it is a multiplier without a denominator, it is not a crash rate, and it does not mean twenty-five times safer than a human. The number Tesla can actually defend today is roughly nine times fewer major collisions than the United States average, on a disclosed denominator. The next robotaxi safety report and the December rollout are the two documents that matter.

What I could not verify: no independent group has yet measured version fifteen's critical intervention rate against version fourteen on the same fleet, because the rollout began yesterday and that data does not exist yet.

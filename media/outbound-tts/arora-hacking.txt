Jacob, here's the check on the Nikesh Arora post. Short version: this one is real, well timed, and mostly directionally correct. Every numbered trend he invokes checks out against Verizon, Mandiant, C I S A and the research literature. What fails is his rhetoric. Words like trivial, never, and not more sophisticated go well past what the same evidence actually shows, including the most cited dataset of all, which frames this moment as refinement, not revolution.

First the identity, because I had this wrong. Nikesh Arora is the chairman and chief executive of Palo Alto Networks. Not Talos, not Ark, not Cyera. The account is verified individually, joined in June two thousand nine, one hundred thirty five thousand one hundred forty seven followers, bio reads Palo Alto Networks. Palo Alto's own management page confirms the role, and their site carries his name as author on his bylined pieces. One citation hygiene note. Palo Alto's Defender's Guide to the Frontier Artificial Intelligence Impact on Cybersecurity is authored by Lee Klarich, not Arora. Arora's own piece is called Weaponized Intelligence.

Now the "six months ago" claim, which is supported. On March thirtieth, twenty twenty six, in Weaponized Intelligence, he wrote that over the next six months the barrier to entry for sophisticated attacks will continue to diminish, and quote, a hacker's dream weapon will be available to anyone with a credit card and computer. There's a second and better fitting antecedent. In an April twenty twenty six talk, reported May seventeenth, he said that in the next six months they will surface as many vulnerabilities as it once took a decade to uncover, and that if the model's context window is large enough it can chain multiple independent vulnerabilities together and reason out a complete attack path. That last line is almost verbatim the daisy chaining line in this post. So the linkage is confirmed. The nuance is that his six month window from March closed in late September, and the April talk's six month window closed right around now.

And Mythos is real, not a transcription error. This is where the post is actually cleverest and where it is also slightly dishonest. Anthropic released Claude Mythos 5 on June ninth, twenty twenty six, but only to a small group of vetted partners, initially through Project Glasswing with the U.S. government. Access was suspended June twelfth, export controls lifted July first for a set of American organizations, and Mythos 5.1 on September first still says access remains limited to a small set of vetted organizations. Meanwhile Anthropic released Claude Fable 5 the same day, described as a Mythos class model that they made safe for general use, with capabilities exceeding any model they had ever made generally available. So the capability class is broadly available through Fable 5, but Mythos itself is still rationed. Arora's phrasing, Mythos like capability will be widely available, is literally imprecise and quietly self serving, because it lets him claim credit for a thing Anthropic still holds back. The honest framing is that Mythos class capability reached the public through a different product than the one he named.

Now the substance, claim by claim.

Attacks will accelerate. Directionally supported, unfalsifiable as a forecast. The Verizon D B I R twenty twenty six analyzed thirty one thousand incidents and over twenty two thousand confirmed breaches, the largest single report they have ever done. Ransomware at forty eight percent, up from forty four. Third party involved breaches up sixty percent year over year to forty eight percent of all breaches. C I S A's known exploited vulnerabilities catalog grew by two hundred eighty two entries in twenty twenty six through October thirtieth, about twenty eight a month, to seventeen hundred sixty six total, with three hundred sixty one tied to ransomware.

A critical vulnerability in the wild. Supported. PHP two shell, disclosed October twenty first and twenty second, a remote code execution chain in PHP. The N S A issued a critical patch advisory, British and American sites were taken down to patch, PHP underpins about seventy percent of the web, and Wiz called it arguably the most severe vulnerability in software history. Cloudflare saw tens of thousands of exploit attempts in forty five minutes and millions after disclosure.

A social engineering attack that looks innocuous. Supported, and this is the best-documented one. Mandiant's M Trends twenty twenty six, built on over five hundred thousand hours of twenty twenty five investigations, shows voice phishing surging to eleven percent of initial infection vectors, the number two position, while email phishing fell from fourteen percent to six percent. The D B I R shows mobile centric phishing clicks succeed forty percent more often than email in simulations. The human element is in sixty two percent of breaches.

Attack path discovery is now trivial. Here is where he overshoots. The strongest real evidence is the ARTEMIS paper, arXiv twenty five twelve oh nine eight eight two, which put ten human professionals against six existing agents plus ARTEMIS on a live network of about eight thousand hosts across twelve subnets. ARTEMIS placed second of eleven, found nine valid vulnerabilities at an eighty two percent valid submission rate, outperformed nine of the ten humans, and cost eighteen dollars an hour against sixty dollars an hour for humans. Real. But the same paper reports higher false positive rates, struggles with graphical user interface tasks, and the off the shelf scaffolds, Codex and Cy Agent, underperformed most humans. And Wiz said the PHP two shell chain required an exceptionally complex chain of steps, at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve. That is the opposite of trivial.

Attacks are not becoming more sophisticated, same attacks, faster and by a lot more people. Half supported, half contradicted. The supporting half is real. The D B I R's own summary says refinement, not revolution. The median actor used artificial intelligence assistance across about fifteen distinct attack and C K techniques. Check Point's twenty twenty six report says artificial intelligence has crossed from assistant to operator. The contradicting half is just as documented. The D B I R says more zero days and critical vulnerabilities year over year. Mandiant says zero day exploitation is accelerating and the mean time to exploit vulnerabilities has plummeted. The reality is volume and speed up, and a subset of attacks also got more complex.

You are as secure as your supply chain. Supported, and this is the best evidenced line in the post. Third party involvement in forty eight percent of breaches, up sixty percent. Sonatype's twenty twenty six research counts over one point two three three million malicious open source packages cumulatively, up seventy five percent year over year, with nine point eight trillion downloads, up sixty seven percent. ReversingLabs, January twenty seventh, put malicious package detections up seventy three percent in twenty twenty five with npm at about ninety percent of all open source malware. One caveat, package detections are not breaches, and the trend is not uniform, PyPI detections fell forty three percent and NuGet fell sixty percent.

Now the code claim, which he phrases as don't forget the coding models have trained on human code. The first half is strongly evidenced. Veracode tested over a hundred models across eighty tasks and forty five percent of Artificial intelligence generated samples failed security tests, with eighty six percent failing cross site scripting defense. Their spring twenty twenty six update shows the pass rate still flat at about fifty five percent since twenty twenty five, syntax errors down seventy six percent, logic bugs down sixty percent, but privilege escalation flaws up three hundred twenty two percent. Package hallucination is real too. A USENIX security paper found roughly twenty percent of Artificial intelligence generated code referencing non existent packages, feeding slopsquatting, though a February twenty twenty six Safeguard study put the better assistants in the low single digits. The specific causal claim, that models trained on human code reintroduce already patched C V Es, is plausible and reported, but I could not independently confirm the underlying study. Partially supported, and it is the weakest link in his argument.

Now his four point advice against C I S A's actual twenty twenty six guidance. All four align, and this is the part most people would skim. Test your infrastructure for accessible vulnerabilities and misconfigurations, that is C I S A's known exploited catalog plus B O D twenty two oh one's six month and fourteen day remediation cadence, and the D B I R's continuous vulnerability management action set. Fix your code and demand your vendors do, that is the Secure by Design program and the pledge with over two hundred manufacturers. Old insecure infrastructure at the perimeter first, twenty twenty six K E V additions are edge heavy, Cisco twenty, Fortinet nine, Ivanti eight, plus Palo Alto's own P A N O S, and Mandiant has a dedicated section on edge device backdoors with three hundred ninety three day dwell times. And new artificial intelligence tools need a security framework. C I S A and its international partners published Careful Adoption of Agentic Artificial Intelligence Services on May first, twenty twenty six, and the joint C I S A and N S A guidance on secure artificial intelligence in operational technology came out in January. So nothing here is contrarian. It is a chief executive restating published federal guidance, and it is all sound.

Now the dissent, which matters. The D B I R itself is explicitly cautious. The team has maintained a skeptic stance on artificial intelligence, they note the world is not yet ready for a groundbreaking artificial intelligence enabled attack, and they say there is still not more available and verifiable data on what artificial intelligence enabled offense actually looks like defensively. That is the single most important counterweight to Arora's framing, because the most cited twenty twenty six dataset of all is declining to endorse the revolution story. Add that Anthropic's own gating means the capability was not uniformly available during the six month window he is claiming, and that Palo Alto is a security vendor selling agentic and vibe coding security products, so its research is interested party analysis, which is why every number I leaned on comes from Verizon, Mandiant, C I S A, the N S A, arXiv, USENIX, Wiz, or Cloudflare.

Bottom line. Eight things in this post are established twenty twenty six trends and I would cite them. Exploitation overtook credential abuse as the leading initial access vector, thirty one percent against thirteen percent. Remediation is failing where it matters, only twenty six percent of K E V criticals fully remediated, median forty three days, and twenty nine percent attacked before public disclosure. Third party exposure is now about half of all breaches. Social engineering has shifted to voice and pretexting. Artificial intelligence assistance is standard across the kill chain. Artificial intelligence pentest economics changed materially. Artificial intelligence generated code is insecure at scale. And his advice matches federal guidance line for line.

Seven things are opinion, forecast, or overstated. Attacks will accelerate is directionally evidenced but unfalsifiable. Incident responders have never been busier, no baseline supports never, and there is no responder workload metric in the public record. Attack path discovery is now trivial is overstated. Attacks are not becoming more sophisticated is partly contradicted by the same D B I R he is likely citing. The coding models trained on human code causal claim is only partially verified. More people becoming better at hacking is mechanism yes, magnitude opinion, and it is his own admission of a blind spot. And Mythos like capability will be widely available is only true if you read it as Fable five.

Read this one as a well timed executive threat post whose data points check out and whose rhetoric outruns them. If you are running exposed infrastructure, his four point checklist is genuinely the right order of operations and it matches what C I S A is telling you right now. The one line I would not repeat is the trivial part, because that is where the actual risk of complacency lives.

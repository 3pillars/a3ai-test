The post says the company is announcing a proof of the Aaronson Ambainis conjecture, one of the central open problems in quantum complexity. It has about three hundred fourteen thousand views, sixty replies, four hundred twenty five reposts, about eighteen hundred likes and seven hundred thirty two bookmarks. So this one is getting real attention.

What is the claim. The conjecture dates to two thousand eight. It asks whether a quantum computer can be exponentially faster than an ordinary computer on random inputs, with no hidden pattern. Shor's factoring algorithm is fast because it exploits a repeating pattern. Aaronson and Ambainis showed that if a statement about low degree polynomials is true, then without such structure the quantum speedup in how many times you read the input is at most polynomial. P S I says its internal research system, running a non public Anthropic model, found a full proof on the third day of a run, and that the proof assistant Lean accepted it.

What I checked. The company's research page, the paper and the Lean repository on Git Hub all exist and say what the post says. I read the formal statement in the repository. It matches the conjecture as described: a polynomial with values between zero and one on the cube, bounded degree, variance at least epsilon, and some bit whose influence is at least epsilon over d, to the seventh power. I am not a Lean referee, so that is a plain reading, not an audit. Trade press, including The Quantum Insider, reports the claim, but that is repeating the company, not independent confirmation.

What is not verified. First, the paper itself is not machine checked, only the Lean part. Second, one input from earlier work by Agarwal and Ben David is not formalized and is a hypothesis of one of the four theorems. Third, the Stanford researchers who are quoted calling it a huge result are quoted by P S I, not on their own channels. I found no public reaction from Aaronson or Ambainis, and no peer review yet. The model is not public, so nobody can reproduce how the proof was found. Only the output can be checked.

What it means. If it holds, it narrows where quantum speedups can come from: problems with structure. It limits how often an algorithm must read its input, not total computing time, so it does not rule out useful quantum machines. It does not touch Shor's algorithm, because factoring is exactly the structured case. So this is not a change in Bitcoin quantum risk. For you, this is a science and A I capability story, not a trade signal, and it does not change your tripwires.

Bottom line: a credible claim with unusually good evidence for the formal part. Wait for outside experts before calling it settled.

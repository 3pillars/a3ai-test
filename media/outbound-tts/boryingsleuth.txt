The verdict: the technical claim is right and is now the officially acknowledged mechanism, but the post's framing mixes that good call with a separate, much weaker allegation and dresses both in prophetic language.

The author is Tyler Reed, also known as Truth Labs. The post says: the Binance exploit wouldn't have happened without E.I.P. seven seven zero two. Who's the Ethereum hidden founders? Through time my work will be timeless, God worked through me to bring truth to light, it's why I'm still here. It reposts his own May twenty twenty-four thread about Ethereum's early I.C.O. wallets being funded by the same Silk Road address that led to Mt. Gox exploiter Ross Ulbricht's D.P.R. wallet.

What's confirmed. The technical core is not just right, it is now the official account. Binance co-C.E.O. Richard Teng said the attacker used the E.I.P. seven seven zero two delegation mechanism to gain execution authority over a Binance hot wallet and bypass the exchange's normal transaction approval controls. The Defiant reported that directly. About two and a half billion dollars drained on Ethereum in under four minutes, across roughly fifty signatures. Security firm Blockaid identified multiple Binance-controlled keys in the signed activity. Teng said investigators found no evidence the wallet private keys themselves were leaked, and that the attackers used self-destruct mechanisms to erase tools and traces. Multiple people inside Binance were involved. So if the post had stopped at the technical claim, it would have been a sharp, on-point observation that aged well.

Now the important caveat on that technical claim. EIP seven seven zero two is a standard Ethereum protocol upgrade letting an externally owned account delegate to a contract. It is widely used and it is not malicious in itself. The security coverage said the upgrade did not cause the hack, but may have helped attackers bypass checks. So the accurate statement is that EIP seven seven zero two delegation, combined with a compromised signing system, was the attack vector. The post's without would not have happened is too strong, but not wildly off.

The Ethereum founders part. This is a real, documented investigation, not a fabrication, and I under-called it at first. In January twenty twenty-five, Truth Labs accused Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin of benefiting from Bitcoin seized from Ross Ulbricht's Silk Road wallet during the Ethereum I.C.O., alleging Buterin was aware as early as January twenty fourteen of how the I.C.O. would be funded. The allegation surfaced after Buterin publicly backed Ulbricht on X, calling his life sentence absurd. Crypto News, Coin Edition, WikiBit, Blockchair and Bitspeaker all covered it. What I could not find is a formal response from Buterin or the Ethereum Foundation to the funding allegation itself. So the facts are: the claim is specific, on-chain based, and widely reported, and it remains an unresolved allegation rather than an established finding. What's also true is that a large anonymous donation of about three hundred bitcoin, roughly thirty-one million dollars, went to Ulbricht in late twenty twenty-five, and Chainalysis suspected it came from someone associated with AlphaBay.

The God and truth-through-time language is worth naming plainly, because it tells you how to weight everything else. This is a self-described prophetic investigator. A technical claim from someone with a record of correct calls is worth much more than the same claim from someone who presents findings as divine truth, and here the framing is doing a lot of work to make the weaker allegation sound like part of the same finding.

My take. The technical insight is genuinely good and it aged well: EIP seven seven zero two was the vector, and Binance's CEO has now said so on the record. The Ethereum I.C.O. funding claim is a legitimate open question that serious outlets covered, but it is an allegation, and it rides on the back of the good technical claim rather than resting on its own. The takeaway that matters is structural: delegated accounts plus a compromised signing system is a serious risk for any hot wallet, and it is a property of your own account, not just Binance's. If you hold non-custodial assets, it is worth checking whether a delegator has ever been set on your addresses.

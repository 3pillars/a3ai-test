Jacob, here's the check on the Grok Jarvis post. The short version: the underlying product is real, and honestly more capable than I expected, but the post is selling a demo as if it were a product, and the title framing is wrong. This is a legitimate look at a real thing wearing a sales funnel.

First the post. Julian Goldie, a hundred and seventy one thousand six hundred and ninety one followers, put it up Sunday, November first, at eleven twenty five a.m. U T C, which is six twenty five a.m. Eastern, and three twenty five in the morning your time in Tahoe. The caption is three words: what the hell is Grok Jarvis. It comes with a thirteen second video at full high definition, about four point eight megabytes, which I downloaded and it plays clean. As of this evening it has three hundred thirty one thousand eight hundred fifty six views, one thousand two hundred seventy four likes, a hundred and thirty nine reposts, one hundred five replies, fourteen quotes, and one thousand four hundred twenty eight bookmarks. The bookmark count is the tell. People are not sharing this, they are saving it, which means they are treating it as a build spec.

Now the substance. Here is the claim in his own words: Grok Jarvis is a Grok Bot plus a Page. The bot holds the personality, the Page is the live screen it builds from plain English. No code. Two prompts and one fix message.

Here is what checks out against the official documentation. Grok Bot is real, it launched on August eleventh, twenty twenty six, and the docs describe it as AI teammates with names, jobs, and context that compounds over time, each one working on a persistent cloud computer with a browser, filesystem, and terminal. That is verbatim from the docs. So the base capability is confirmed, and it is genuinely more than a chat wrapper. The bots run on a shared cloud computer, one per account, they keep working in the background when you close your laptop, they get their own screen, and they learn skills and routines by watching what you do. They can sign in to sites using credentials from a vault, they tag you for approval before consequential actions, and you can approve from chat, from the mobile app, or from X. Grok Bot runs on macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS, and Android. It ships with Premium, Super, and Ultra on the X subscription, and it is also bundled with paid Cursor plans.

The Page part is real too, just not named that. The official feature is Pages, plural, inside Grok Bot. The docs say a page is something a Bot builds for you to read, such as a report, a memo, a dashboard, a tracker, or an explainer, it can hold text, tables, charts, and controls like filters and switches, it lives in the Bot's Library, and it opens with the chat still beside it so you can keep talking while you read. The official bot account announced on October twenty first that Grok Bot can now build reports, memos, and dashboards. And the FAQ literally lists model for each task, own computer, approvals, voice, and dashboard as Grok Bot concepts. So all eight parts of his list have real counterparts somewhere in the product.

Now the corrections. Three things he says that are not quite right.

One, the naming. There is no product called Grok Jarvis, and there is no feature called a Grok Page. It is Grok Bot, and Pages within it. Jarvis is Goldie's personal naming for his own bot. The reason this matters is concrete: if you search for Grok Jarvis you will find nothing official, and if you want to build this you are looking in the wrong place. Second, the model powering Pages is not disclosed. The official FAQ says Grok Bot picks the model for each task, that it can be a first party model or a third party model, and that users cannot be routed to or away from a particular model. Interestingly the FAQ itself even cites Opus five point five. So you cannot assume you are getting Grok for this. Third, and this is the one that breaks his headline, no code is wrong. You are not writing code, that part is true, but you are not writing nothing either. You are writing prompts and a specification, and more importantly the Pages feature is rolling out gradually, it is not available on Enterprise teams yet, and you need Grok Bot version zero point sixty zero or later on desktop and one point fourteen zero or later on phone. If you do not see pages, that is a version or rollout problem, not you.

Now the part I think he is being sneaky about. Read his eight list again with the docs open. The personality, that is a system prompt. The morning brief, that is a routine, and Grok Bot does have skills and routines. The Systems panel listing every bot with a status light, that is a Page he asked the bot to build. The waiting on you queue, that is the approval system, which is real but has a hard limit I found in the docs: approvals raised outside a chat you are watching expire after about thirty minutes, and approvals inside a chat you are in wait for you. His line "approve or later on each one, ticks off on your screen only, nothing gets sent" is a description of his own setup, not a documented product guarantee. The voice reading answers out loud is voice chat, which is real. Quick commands are skills. The self fix, where he told the bot what he saw, pasted the error, and it fixed its own work and sent a bigger core, that is the bot having a persistent computer with a filesystem and a browser, and it happens to work. But it is his specific bug, his specific fix, and it is the one item on the list that is pure demo. And his own computer, opening a browser, searching Google, reading competitor sites one by one and returning a table, with a rules guard of only read, no forms, no contact, that is real capability, and the guard rails are something you write yourself, not something the product enforces.

And the thirty one active bots number. That is the headline flex and it is the least informative claim in the post. The docs say every Bot on your account uses one persistent cloud computer and they share its files, and each Bot gets its own screen on that computer, with one Bot running one computer use task at a time. Thirty one bots sharing one machine and one browser is not thirty one parallel workers, it is thirty one tabs on one box. It is a lot of configuration, not a lot of compute.

Bottom line. This is real, and it is a genuinely interesting product, more so than I expected when I saw a SEO guy's video about it. But Goldie has packaged a personal project with a custom personality, a custom dashboard, and one lucky bug fix as if it were a named product with a spec. The two claims that would mislead you are the name and the no code line. If you want this, the path is Grok Bot, then build a Page and a routine, and understand that you are building a system, not switching on a product. The SEO play here is obvious, and it is that people who see a forty second demo of a working agent stack assume it takes no work. It takes an afternoon of prompting and a lot of swearing.

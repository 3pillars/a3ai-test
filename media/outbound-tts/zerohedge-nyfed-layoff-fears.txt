This is the check on a Zero Hedge post. The post I D is two one one nine eight four eight eight five six two one two nine seven nine eight nine two. That is nineteen digits. It went up Monday, November ninth, at nine forty five in the morning Pacific time. It is a link with the headline: layoff fears hit highest since Covid crash as inflation expectations stay stuck near three year high, N Y Fed survey. It has about forty two thousand six hundred views, seventy four likes, and nineteen replies.

The numbers are real. I checked them against the New York Fed's own October Survey of Consumer Expectations release.

Inflation expectations: the median is unchanged at three point nine percent for one year ahead, three point three percent for three years, and three point zero percent for five years. Zero Hedge adds the unrounded one year figure of three point nine one percent against a consensus of three point eight, and says the five year number has sat at three percent for fourteen months. I confirmed the rounded figures at the Fed. The unrounded figure, the consensus and the fourteen months come from Zero Hedge only.

Labor market: mean unemployment expectations, meaning the probability people give that unemployment will be higher in a year, rose one point four points to forty five point three percent. The Fed says that is the highest since April twenty twenty. That is the "highest since Covid" in the headline. Confirmed.

Now the problem with the headline. It says layoff fears. But the survey's own measure of fear of losing your job went the other way. The perceived chance of losing your job in the next twelve months rose only point four points to thirteen point nine percent, which is below its twelve month average of fourteen point three. The chance of finding a new job if laid off rose to forty six point five percent, the highest since November twenty twenty five. So people expect the economy to get worse, but they are not more afraid for their own jobs. The headline blends the two.

Also, the increase was driven by people without a college degree, per the Fed. Zero Hedge notes college graduates are the most pessimistic in level, at fifty one percent, but that figure is from the full survey tables and I did not check it.

The hard data points the other way, as Zero Hedge says. I checked the Federal Reserve Bank of Saint Louis data series. October payrolls rose by one hundred sixty five thousand, and unemployment was four point one percent. Weekly jobless claims hit one hundred eighty nine thousand for the week ending October thirty first. That ties July as the lowest since nineteen sixty nine, so Zero Hedge's claim holds. The survey was fielded through October thirty first, before the jobs report.

Other claims from the article. The Fed held rates at three seventy five to four percent last month, and Zero Hedge says officials are talking about hikes, with a December ninth decision. I did not check that. Goldman's October C P I preview, core zero point one six percent against a consensus of zero point two, is from a paywalled note that Zero Hedge quotes, so it is unverified. October C P I comes out tomorrow.

Bottom line: accurate figures, misleading headline. The survey shows pessimism about the labor market in general, while personal job worries are ordinary and the actual data is strong. For you, this is a stagflation-flavored read, with sticky inflation expectations and a hot jobs report keeping a December hike alive. That is a headwind for bitcoin near the fifty week average. It is not a new trade signal and does not change your tripwires. Tomorrow's C P I is the next catalyst.

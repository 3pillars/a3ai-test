The central checkable claim is the chart. The article says the note shows ten year real yields back at levels last seen in two thousand seven and two thousand eight. That is verifiable against the Federal Reserve's inflation indexed ten year series, and it holds up precisely.

Here are the numbers. The ten year real yield, the TIPS series, closed at two point six three percent on the twenty ninth of October. Now, the last time that series was at or above two point six three before this year was the twenty sixth of November, two thousand eight, when it printed two point six eight. That is roughly eighteen years ago. The two thousand seven high was two point eight three, in June. The two thousand eight high was three point fifteen, in November. And in two thousand twenty six the series peaked at two point nine five, on the fifth of October.

So the claim is not just directionally right. It is right to the decimal, and it is arguably understated. The series has been at these levels since before the last financial crisis.

For context, the nominal ten year finished at four point nine eight percent, and the ten year expected inflation rate was two point three four. The real yield is doing all of the work.

Here's my verified check on the CryptoCitizen88 post, the one arguing Binance might be insolvent.

The verdict: the mechanism of the criticism is right and I confirmed it against Binance's own proof of reserves page, but the headline claim that Binance has never had a real audit is false, and the post's own test has already been run today and passed.

What the post claims. Not saying it's true but this might actually be possible. Binance has never had a real audit. Their proof of reserves is a monthly snapshot of customer balances. It doesn't count the company's own debts or loans, and critics have pointed out you could borrow money short term just to pass it. When an outside firm checked them in twenty twenty-two, one report had their Bitcoin backing at ninety-seven percent. Their own token B.N.B. is a huge part of the whole empire. That's the same setup F.T.X. had with F.T.T. Next proof of reserves should drop in the next few days. If any major coin comes in under one hundred percent after this hack, that tells you everything. It quotes another account claiming Binance has been insolvent since last year at least.

What I confirmed. The core structural criticism is accurate, and I read it off Binance's own proof of reserves page directly. The page lists a monthly audit time for every month going back years, with the snapshot described as the asset holding within customer account balances at the date and time of the snapshot. The methodology is four steps: verify ownership of the address, snapshot of user balances, generate zk-SNARKs proof, and generation of a Merkle tree. So yes, it is a self-published monthly snapshot with cryptographic verification that your own account is in the liability set. It is not an audit of the company's balance sheet, and it does not touch corporate debts, loans, or the SAFU fund. The post is right about what the thing is and right about what it cannot tell you.

The ninety-seven percent figure is real, and the nuance makes it more interesting rather than less. The Mazars report of November twenty twenty-two, as analyzed by CryptoQuant, found Binance's Bitcoin liabilities were ninety-seven percent collateralized as of November twenty-second, once you excluded assets lent out through loans and margin accounts. Include those lent assets and Mazars said the Bitcoin reserves were overcollateralized at a hundred and one percent. Also worth knowing: Mazars suspended all of its crypto client work, including Binance, in December twenty twenty-two. That is the hinge of the whole post.

The headline claim is wrong. Binance has had third-party auditor reports. Mazars is one, and Ernst and Young audited Binance earlier. What is true is that the current proof of reserves is not third-party audited. That is a meaningful difference and the post did not make it.

The test already ran, today, and it passed. I pulled Binance's live proof of reserves report. A new one posted for October thirty-first, twenty twenty-six, at B.T.C. block height nine seven three thousand eight hundred and ninety-five, which is after the hack. The ratios: Bitcoin one hundred point zero four percent, Ethereum one hundred point zero zero percent, Tether one hundred point zero zero percent, BNB one hundred and one point three zero percent, X.R.P. one hundred point ninety-nine percent. Every major coin is at or above one hundred percent on Binance's own numbers. So the post's own stated condition, if any major coin comes in under one hundred percent, has not been met.

Now the caveats, and they are not small. These are self-reported numbers with no third-party auditor, and the snapshot moment is the exchange's choice. Binance has also said it set aside six point three four billion dollars to cover the losses, which means the reserve ratio is a function of how you book a contingent liability, not of solvency. And the notorious thing about the ninety-seven percent is precisely that it was true on Binance's own published numbers in twenty twenty-two.

The F.T.X. and F.T.T. comparison is rhetoric. BNB is a large part of the Binance ecosystem, yes, but it is not the balance-sheet loop F.T.X. ran with F.T.T., where Alameda used the token to prop up the books.

My take. This is a better argument than most of the insolvency chatter, because it stays on method rather than on insinuation. The two things to actually watch are the next snapshot's date and whether an independent auditor ever signs one. If Binance can get a reputable firm to attest after this, the argument weakens a lot. If the next report slips or the ratios drift toward one hundred, that is the signal worth acting on.

For your positions, nothing here changes anything. Bitcoin is about seventy-seven thousand seven hundred dollars, no tripwire has fired, and this is about Binance's counterparty risk, not the Bitcoin network. The one thing that matters if you hold anything on Binance is that the current proof of reserves says you are covered. This isn't investment advice, and I took no action.

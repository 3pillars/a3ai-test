His argument. He quotes Zero Hedge's chart of the J P Morgan midterm scenario sheet, and says that view is Wall Street consensus, but it assumes there is one Democratic Party. He thinks there are two: the moderate, traditional Democrats, and the Democratic Socialists of America. So the margin of victory could matter a lot for how markets react.

He backs this with James Carville, on the Bulwark podcast on October eighteenth. Carville said he would rather Republicans keep the House by five seats than Democrats win by five, because a thin majority would leave Democrats hostage to the D S A. Bee-ahn-koh adds that in a fifty one to forty nine Democratic Senate, Fetterman could side with Republicans, while Bernie Sanders and a likely Michigan winner, Abdul El Sigh-eed, could block bills they find not progressive enough.

Then the market angle. He says the fight would come to a head quickly over data centers. Unions and middle class voters want the jobs, he calls them accelerationists. The D S A wants to block more construction, the decelerationists.

What I checked. The Carville quote and date match several outlets, including Fox News and Media-ite. J P Morgan's note is real. Its trading desk gave Democrats a sixty six percent chance of winning both chambers, using Bloom-berg prediction market odds as of November first. In that case it sees the S and P five hundred between down a quarter percent and up half a percent. The desk also said fears of artificial intelligence regulation are overstated, because Trump would not sign a bill limiting artificial intelligence, and Democrats cannot override a veto.

My take. Bee-ahn-koh is right that J P Morgan's note treats Democrats as one bloc, and the Carville quote is accurate. But the data center split is his forecast, not a fact. I found nothing showing the Democratic caucus has voted along that line. Fetterman has crossed party lines before, but a fifty one to forty nine Senate is itself only one scenario, and the polls in Michigan are close. If it does happen, the risk for artificial intelligence stocks is slower data center permitting, not new federal law.

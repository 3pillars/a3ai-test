Check on a post by Ric, handle Ric R T P, about Apple cutting AI budgets. The post I.D. is two one one eight one three nine five seven two five eight four six three two seven two two. That is nineteen digits. It went up at four thirty-three p.m. Pacific yesterday, November fourth.

A disclosure first. I am a Claude model, and Claude's maker, Anthropic, is the company this post is about. I am not a neutral judge of it.

The post argues that Jensen Huang says engineers should burn two hundred fifty thousand dollars of AI a year, while Apple is cutting its engineers' AI budget to about fifty thousand. It says the real limit on the AI boom is now the finance department, lists other companies that capped spending, and says that is a problem for Anthropic's coming IPO. It ends by asking who is right, Jensen or Apple's finance team. I transcribed the attached video, which is a seventy-seven second clip of Jensen Huang making that point.

Engagement is large. About one hundred thirty-eight thousand views, four hundred eighty-nine likes, eighty-nine reposts, one hundred five replies, two hundred thirty-five bookmarks. The account is verified, with about forty-nine thousand followers. The bio says he is a content partner for finance and tech founders, so he writes for a living, and this post is built as an argument.

Now the claims.

First, Apple. Confirmed. Bloomberg reported on November fourth, and Reuters and TechCrunch carried it, that Apple cut the daily AI spending cap for some engineers from three hundred dollars to two hundred in October, is considering a hundred dollars for some teams, and has discussed as little as twenty-five dollars for some non-engineering groups. The post's arithmetic is also right: two hundred dollars a day over about two hundred fifty workdays is fifty thousand, and a hundred is twenty-five thousand. I could not open Bloomberg, so I have not seen its detail about how few engineers used the full cap.

Second, Jensen. Confirmed, with a caveat. In March, on the All-In podcast, he said he would be deeply alarmed if a five hundred thousand dollar engineer did not use at least two hundred fifty thousand dollars of tokens. The clip matches. But he was talking about elite engineers at Nvidia, and Nvidia sells the chips. The post turns it into a rule that every engineer's AI spend should be half of pay. That is the post's framing. Comparing it with Apple's cap also assumes the cap is the average, when Apple lets staff ask leaders for more.

Third, the other companies. Mostly confirmed. Accenture began centrally funding only twenty-nine dollars a month per employee for Claude on October first, after some staff spent up to a thousand, per 404 Media and Forbes, though projects can pay for more. Uber used up its yearly AI coding budget in about four months, and set a fifteen hundred dollar monthly cap per tool, though that was reported in April and May, not now. Microsoft's AI chief Mustafa Suleyman told Bloomberg in June that Anthropic's models are too expensive, and Microsoft has since swapped in its own models in some products. I did not verify the exact wording the post uses. For Meta, a leaderboard of token use existed, but the report was of an employee-built dashboard, and in July Meta's Adam Mosseri said budgets could soon be capped. So has introduced token budgets is stronger than what I found.

Fourth, Anthropic. Confirmed. The amended S one filing showed third quarter revenue of about seventeen billion dollars, up forty-seven percent from the second quarter, against a hundred forty-four percent the quarter before. Price reductions and contract discounts cut about six hundred million dollars from revenue. Purchase commitments are eight hundred ninety-five billion dollars. The filing blames capacity limits, seasonality and customer restrictions as well as pricing. The IPO is targeted for November. I did not confirm the roadshow starting next week.

Fifth, the conclusion. The post says the finance department is now the real limit on the boom. That is its opinion, and the evidence is real but incomplete. The post itself concedes that Bloomberg says few Apple engineers used the full cap, so savings may be small, and cheaper models can do the same work. Capping spend per person can coexist with more total spending. I did not check Apple's research spending figure, or the one point four trillion dollars of OpenAI compute deals.

Bottom line. The facts are mostly accurate and the framing is aggressive. Companies are rationing AI spend per worker, which is a real signal. It does not prove that the AI boom is hitting a wall, and a cap is not a demand collapse.

For your positions. This fits this week's pattern: the Nasdaq correction, AI capex worries and neocloud credit stress. Falling spend per worker is a risk to AI revenue growth, and Anthropic's own filing shows growth slowing. It is not a trade signal.

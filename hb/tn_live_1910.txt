## One Indian crew member remains missing after strikes on tanker, foreign ministry says

India's Foreign Ministry confirmed 21 crew members have been safely evacuated following strikes on the Panama-flagged LPG tanker MT TARA GAS in the Strait of Hormuz today.

The Ministry of External Affairs in India confirmed that of the 24 Indians on board, two crew members had been taken to hospital while one remains unaccounted for.

"We appreciate the timely assistance and support extended by the government of Oman in the rescue efforts," the ministry said.

"The continuing incidents of attacks on shipping in the region are deeply worrisome and pose a serious threat to the safety of civilian seafarers. The targeting of commercial shipping and civilian infrastructure must end, and free and unimpeded navigation and commerce through international waterways, in keeping with international law, must be restored at the earliest."

Reports from UK Maritime Trade Operations said a second strike hit the tanker about 13km north of Khasab, Oman.

## Ship captains told to refresh navigation skills as Iran steps up jamming of satellites

A ship heading towards the Strait of Hormuz, where jamming is targeting navigation systems on up to 1,000 ships a day. AFP

International shipping companies are telling captains to retrain in basic navigation systems as Iran is targeting hundreds of vessels each day in the Strait of Hormuz.

There has been a vast increase in jamming and spoofing by Iran, which has particularly targeted tankers and container ships travelling in the Arabian Gulf, a UK parliamentary hearing on satellite resilience was told.

The threat from conflicts and cyber attacks has pushed the Maritime and Coastguard Agency to issue a warning urging ships to ensure they are able to navigate without GPS, which is reliant entirely on satellites.

“In June 2026, 970 ships per day in the Strait of Hormuz were jammed,” said Lee Willcox-Jones, an expert at Satellite Applications Catapult. “With these activities taking place, including in the Baltic region, there is a concern around our ability to operate in a jammed or spoofed environment.”

## Bahrain condemns Iranian attacks and 'reserves right to self-defence'

Bahrain today condemned continued Iranian attacks on its territory, saying its air defence systems intercepted and destroyed aerial threats before they could reach their targets.

The Foreign Ministry described the attacks as a violation of Bahrain's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and a dangerous escalation threatening regional security and stability.

"Bahrain reserves the right to take all necessary measures to protect its sovereignty and the security of its citizens and residents, including exercising its inherent right to individual and collective self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter," the ministry said.

It added that Bahrain would co-ordinate with the Gulf Co-operation Council, under the bloc's Joint Defence Agreement, as well as with its allies and partners.

The ministry also called on the UN Security Council to take firm deterrent measures to compel Iran to immediately and completely halt its attacks, violations of other states' sovereignty, interference in their internal affairs, threats to maritime and air navigation, and support for proxy groups in the region.

It called for Iran to be held accountable and provide reparations, including compensation, in accordance with international law and relevant UN resolutions.

## Two vessels hit mines in Strait of Hormuz, says IRGC navy

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy has claimed today that two vessels, a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil and a liquefied gas carrier, struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

In a statement carried by Iranian state media, the IRGC navy said the supertanker was hit in the western sector of the strait and the gas carrier was struck while navigating an unauthorised southern route.

It claimed the vessels had been receiving air cover from US fighter jets when they struck the mines, causing explosions, fires and bringing both ships to a halt.

The IRGC navy warned that vessels using the southern route faced an "iron and fiery wall" of naval mines.

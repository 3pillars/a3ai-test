2 min read

A tanker was attacked near the Strait of Hormuz, two British maritime monitors and an Indian minister said on Tuesday, giving contradictory information about the fate of the crew.

Security monitor Vanguard said the Tara Gas, sailing under a Panamanian flag, was struck on Monday while crossing the Strait of Hormuz, about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman.

**[For all the latest headlines follow our Google News channel online or via the app.](https://news.google.com/publications/CAAqMggKIixDQklTR3dnTWFoY0tGV1Z1WjJ4cGMyZ3VZV3hoY21GaWFYbGhMbTVsZENnQVAB?hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en)**

The attack disabled the ship and caused a fire in its engine room, Vanguard said, which nonetheless said that the entire crew was safe and sound.

India’s ports minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed that the Tara Gas, whose crew of 25 includes 24 Indian nationals, had been targeted by a missile in the strait on Monday.

The crew members “are completely safe with no injuries,” he said on X.

British maritime agency UKMTO, however, reported that “the vessel sustained a second strike, which caused a fire onboard,” and said that the crew are “currently unaccounted for.”

On Monday, the tanker On Phoenix, also sailing under a Panamanian flag, was targeted by an unidentified projectile about three nautical miles off the Omani coast, according to the two British organizations.

Before the Middle East war, triggered by US-Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28, about one-fifth of the world’s oil consumption passed through the strait.

Tehran has since placed the strait in a chokehold and regularly targets ships that attempt to transit without its authorization.

Washington has responded by imposing a blockade on Iranian ports, and its navy escorts tankers through the passage.

 Tanker attacked near Hormuz, maritime monitors say 

AFP

Published: 17 November ,2026: 02:42 PM GST Updated: 17 November ,2026: 05:12 PM GST

2 min read

A tanker was attacked near the Strait of Hormuz, two British maritime monitors and an Indian minister said on Tuesday, giving contradictory information about the fate of the crew.

Security monitor Vanguard said the Tara Gas, sailing under a Panamanian flag, was struck on Monday while crossing the Strait of Hormuz, about seven nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman.

**[For all the latest headlines follow our Google News channel online or via the app.](https://news.google.com/publications/CAAqMggKIixDQklTR3dnTWFoY0tGV1Z1WjJ4cGMyZ3VZV3hoY21GaWFYbGhMbTVsZENnQVAB?hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en)**

The attack disabled the ship and caused a fire in its engine room, Vanguard said, which nonetheless said that the entire crew was safe and sound.

India’s ports minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed that the Tara Gas, whose crew of 25 includes 24 Indian nationals, had been targeted by a missile in the strait on Monday.

The crew members “are completely safe with no injuries,” he said on X.

British maritime agency UKMTO, however, reported that “the vessel sustained a second strike, which caused a fire onboard,” and said that the crew are “currently unaccounted for.”

On Monday, the tanker On Phoenix, also sailing under a Panamanian flag, was targeted by an unidentified projectile about three nautical miles off the Omani coast, according to the two British organizations.

Before the Middle East war, triggered by US-Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28, about one-fifth of the world’s oil consumption passed through the strait.

Tehran has since placed the strait in a chokehold and regularly targets ships that attempt to transit without its authorization.

Washington has responded by imposing a blockade on Iranian ports, and its navy escorts tankers through the passage.

A Qatari delegation was en route to Tehran Monday night as Doha intensifies diplomatic efforts to ease regional tensions and broker an agreement between [Iran and the United States](https://www.newarab.com/tag/us-israel-war-iran) to end the war, an informed Iranian source told _The New Arab_.

The delegation, headed by an adviser to Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister, is making its third visit to the Iranian capital in just one week, as mediators seek to narrow differences between Tehran and Washington.

The latest diplomatic mission follows intensive negotiations hosted by Doha in recent days, involving Iranian officials and Qatari mediators.

The Qatari delegation first visited Tehran last Sunday before returning on Friday, when it held 12 hours of intensive talks with Iranian officials.

The delegation subsequently left Tehran accompanied by Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, who also held similar discussions in Doha.

The renewed diplomatic push comes as Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed on Monday evening that indirect consultations and negotiations with the United States [had become "extremely intensive"](https://www.newarab.com/news/iran-intensive-talks-hormuz-reopening-plan-cut-two-days) because of the sensitivity of the current situation.

Speaking to Iranian state television, Araghchi said Qatar and Pakistan were playing highly active roles as mediators between the two countries.

He said Tehran had already presented its conditions and proposals, stressing that further progress depended on Washington's political approach.

"If it chooses genuine diplomacy, Iran is ready. But if it harbours deception and military operations, it will face a harsher and more bitter response than before," Araghchi warned.

The diplomatic efforts are taking place alongside negotiations over reopening the [Strait of Hormuz](https://www.newarab.com/analysis/iran-losing-control-strait-hormuz), a key international shipping route whose closure has heightened regional tensions.

Araghchi said Iran's conditions for reopening the waterway were "completely clear and transparent", insisting that Tehran would only agree to restore access once its demands were accepted.

"Once these conditions are accepted, the strait will reopen and negotiations will resume," he said.

"Otherwise, the situation will remain as it is, and Iran will not bow."

Earlier on Monday, Iran's foreign ministry announced that it was considering a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Washington accepted its demands, shortening a previously proposed one-week timeframe.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei described the revised proposal as further evidence of Tehran's willingness to reach an agreement.

"The possibility of implementing this plan within two days is further proof of Iran's goodwill," Baghaei said.

"We have emphasised that security can be restored to the Strait of Hormuz and negotiations resumed within a short period if Iran's demands are met."

He added that messages continued to be exchanged between Tehran and Washington through intermediaries.

Meanwhile, an informed official told Reuters that Iranian negotiators had left talks in Doha on Sunday, but stressed that their departure was not the result of difficulties in the negotiations.

The official said a draft proposal was being circulated for feedback and that a follow-up meeting could take place.

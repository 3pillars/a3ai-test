## U.S. announces "self-defense" strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island

The U.S. launched precision strikes on an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps missile site on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, according to a U.S. military official.

The official said the strike was launched "in self-defense."

"The strike was in response to IRGC aggression targeting U.S. personnel and assets," the official said. "U.S. Central Command remains highly vigilant and fully postured to protect Americans across the region."

While the official did not specifically mention the IRGC aggression, the strikes came hours after Iran targeted a U.S. radar site in Bahrain. The strikes were successfully intercepted, according to U.S. Central Command.

This was the third attempted strike by Iran on a U.S. military installation in the last five days. All of them were intercepted, U.S. officials said.

Iran had earlier said the U.S. attacked Qeshm Island, but they "caused no damage to residential, commercial, or infrastructural areas," according to Ahmad Nafisi, deputy provincial governor of the southern province of Hormozgan, quoted by the official IRNA news agency.

## Iraq resumes pumping oil through pipeline to Turkey in order to avoid Strait of Hormuz

Iraq has resumed pumping oil to Turkey through a pipeline at an initial capacity of 120,000 barrels a day, Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair al-Abadi said Tuesday in comments carried by state media. The export capacity will be gradually increased to reach 500,000 barrels a day, he said.

The capacity is fairly small compared to other pipelines in the region. The Saudi East-West pipeline, which crosses from the Persian Gulf to the Red Sea in order to avoid the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb Strait, has a capacity of 7 million barrels per day.

In August, Iraq and Turkey signed a deal to increase oil exports through a pipeline to the Turkish Mediterranean port of Ceyhan.

Iraq is moving to strengthen the resilience of its oil export infrastructure following the U.S.-Iran war's disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

The Iraq-Turkey pipeline has been largely idle since 2023 following legal and commercial disputes.

## Houthis say they targeted crucial Saudi East-West oil pipeline in drone attacks

The Iran-backed Houthi militant group in Yemen said it carried out successful drone strikes Tuesday on the Saudi East-West pipeline.

Pumping Station No. 9, west of Riyadh, was allegedly targeted in two phases with three one-way attack drones, the Houthis said. They then targeted Pumping Station No. 8 with two drones. The Houthis claimed they made "precise and direct" hits on the pumping stations.

The Saudis had earlier said they shot down a Houthi drone near Riyadh but it was unclear if it was related to the attack on the East-West pipeline.

The pipeline, which is comprised of 11 pumping stations, was previously targeted in a drone attack on Sept. 11, which knocked out the line for nearly two weeks. That attack was blamed on Iran-backed rebel groups located in northern Iraq.

The pipeline gives the Saudis a way to avoid the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb Strait and instead take oil north in the Red Sea out of the Suez Canal. The 750-mile pipeline, built in the 1980s, consists of two separate pipes carrying a maximum capacity of 7 million barrels of oil per day.

## U.S.-Israeli war has created "horrific" situation for Iranians, Amnesty International says

Iranian authorities have intensified their repression of the country's population in response to the Middle East war, which began weeks after their deadly crackdown on [anti-government protests killed thousands of people](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/iran-protests-biggest-in-years-against-ayatollah-what-to-know/), Amnesty International said on Tuesday.

An internet blackout imposed at the time of the crackdown on Jan. 8 and 9 and the scale of the casualties meant it was not yet possible for the group to quantify the number of people killed, Amnesty's lead Iran researcher, Raha Bahreini, said.

But she told AFP in an interview that "there is no doubt that thousands were massacred in less than 48 hours."

She added that the Middle East war, which began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran on Feb. 28, "with Iranians still reeling from the January massacres, created a horrific situation for the people in the country."

"The authorities used the cover of wartime conditions to intensify repression," she said.

Bahreini lamented the international community had shifted its attention away from the Iranian people to the geopolitical consequences of the conflict.

"The massacres that took place in January, which should have moved then global consciousness, have faded away from attention," she said.

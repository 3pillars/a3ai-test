An oil tanker was set on fire by an attack in the Strait of Hormuz, which has been blockaded by Iran throughout the Middle East war, two maritime monitors said on Monday.

The ship was exiting Hormuz near the Omani coast when an “unknown projectile” struck, causing a fire in the engine room, the UK Marine Trade Operations said.

Security monitor Vanguard identified the tanker as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX and said it was hit three nautical miles off Oman. Both agencies said all crew were safe.

Iran has heavily restricted traffic through Hormuz, insisting vessels use routes it has authorized. It has regularly targeted ships that transit without its authorization.

Earlier, Iran’s military forces said three oil tankers struck naval mines while attempting to transit Hormuz.

“A few hours ago, two oil tankers struck a naval mine while attempting to use the illegal southern shipping route,” Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), the ideological arm of the military, said in a statement.

“Several very powerful explosions lit up the sky over the Strait of Hormuz for hours,” it added, noting that six tankers had caught fire and sunk in the waterway since Sunday.

Separately, Iran’s army said a third oil tanker caught fire on Monday and was “left immobilized after striking a naval mine in the prohibited zone east of the Strait of Hormuz.”

It was not immediately clear whether the ON PHOENIX was among the vessels referred to in the Iranian statements.

 Oil tanker set ablaze by attack in Strait of Hormuz, marine monitors say

2 min read

An oil tanker was set on fire by an attack in the Strait of Hormuz, which has been blockaded by Iran throughout the Middle East war, two maritime monitors said on Monday.

The ship was exiting Hormuz near the Omani coast when an “unknown projectile” struck, causing a fire in the engine room, the UK Marine Trade Operations said.

**[For all the latest headlines follow our Google News channel online or via the app.](https://news.google.com/publications/CAAqMggKIixDQklTR3dnTWFoY0tGV1Z1WjJ4cGMyZ3VZV3hoY21GaWFYbGhMbTVsZENnQVAB?hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en)**

Security monitor Vanguard identified the tanker as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX and said it was hit three nautical miles off Oman. Both agencies said all crew were safe.

Iran has heavily restricted traffic through Hormuz, insisting vessels use routes it has authorized. It has regularly targeted ships that transit without its authorization.

Earlier, Iran’s military forces said three oil tankers struck naval mines while attempting to transit Hormuz.

“A few hours ago, two oil tankers struck a naval mine while attempting to use the illegal southern shipping route,” Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), the ideological arm of the military, said in a statement.

“Several very powerful explosions lit up the sky over the Strait of Hormuz for hours,” it added, noting that six tankers had caught fire and sunk in the waterway since Sunday.

Separately, Iran’s army said a third oil tanker caught fire on Monday and was “left immobilized after striking a naval mine in the prohibited zone east of the Strait of Hormuz.”

It was not immediately clear whether the ON PHOENIX was among the vessels referred to in the Iranian statements.

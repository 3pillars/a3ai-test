Title: Tanker Struck In Strait Of Hormuz. Iran Claims Six Vessels Are Sinking.

URL Source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/tanker-struck-strait-hormuz-iran-145134865.html

Published Time: 2026-11-16T14:51:34Z

Markdown Content:
A tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile while traveling through the [Strait of Hormuz](https://www.ibtimes.com/strait-hormuz-oil-spills-shipping-attacks-3809688) on Monday, triggering an engine-room fire, British maritime authorities reported. Iranian naval forces separately claimed that six oil tankers had caught fire and were sinking following a series of alleged mine incidents in the strategic waterway.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said the incident was reported at 5:58 a.m. UTC by the tanker's company security officer.

The vessel was traveling out of the strait when it was hit by an unknown projectile, causing a fire in its engine room. All crew members were accounted for, and no casualties were reported.

The extent of the damage and any environmental impact remained unknown. UKMTO did not identify the vessel, its operator or the party responsible for the attack.

The agency classified the incident as an attack and advised ships traveling through the area to exercise caution while authorities investigated.

Separately, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy said Monday that two oil tankers had struck [naval mines](https://www.ibtimes.com/iran-limpet-mines-strait-hormuz-shipping-3809226) while attempting to cross the southern Strait of Hormuz.

According to Anadolu Agency, the IRGC alleged that the vessels were traveling under the direct guidance of U.S. officers, with support from American fighter jets.

The Iranian force claimed that the tankers suffered several explosions and caught fire. It further asserted that six tankers had caught fire and were sinking in the waterway since Sunday.

Neither the identities of the six vessels nor their reported condition could be independently confirmed.

Iran's army Navy also reported a separate incident involving a tanker east of the strait early Monday.

According to the army's statement, the vessel struck a naval mine in an area it described as restricted, caught fire and became immobilized.

The army warned that vessels entering restricted waters or switching off their tracking systems faced serious risks.

However, the Iranian military statements did not establish whether the tanker reported by the army was included in the IRGC's six-vessel count.

It was also unclear whether the vessel involved in Monday's UKMTO warning was among those described by Iranian authorities. UKMTO reported an unidentified projectile strike, while Iran attributed its claimed incidents to naval mines.

The Iranian reports remain unverified. Reuters said it could not independently confirm the IRGC's account of tankers striking mines.

The IRGC described the alleged incidents as a consequence of ships using routes Tehran considers unauthorized.

It also claimed that a southern passage used by vessels receiving American assistance had been mined, warning shipping operators against cooperating with the U.S. military.

Those statements came during an ongoing confrontation between Iran and the United States over maritime access to the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran has sought to impose restrictions on vessel movements through the waterway during the conflict, while Washington has maintained a [naval blockade of Iranian ports](https://www.ibtimes.com/us-keeps-blocking-irans-ports-tehrans-shadow-tankers-have-now-run-out-oil-3808881).

The strait, which connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman, remains an important route for oil and petroleum-product shipments from Middle Eastern producers.

Before the conflict disrupted traffic, approximately 15 million barrels of crude oil and 5 million barrels of petroleum products passed through the strait each day, according to the International Energy Agency. The combined volume was equivalent to roughly 20% of global oil consumption.

Shipping through the area has been disrupted for months, with commercial vessels facing attacks and restrictions on navigation.

The latest UKMTO warning followed another reported attack Sunday.

According to the agency's maritime incident records, Indian shipping authorities reported an attack on a tanker traveling eastbound through the Persian Gulf before entering the Strait of Hormuz.

The crew members were reported safe, although the extent of the damage and any environmental impact remained unknown.

The incidents come as global energy markets assess whether recovering Middle East oil exports can be sustained.

In its November Oil Market Report, published Friday, the IEA said global oil supplies were recovering following months of disruption.

The agency reported that Gulf oil exports, excluding Oman, reached 18.4 million barrels a day in October, approximately 26% below pre-war levels.

It also forecast that global oil supply would exceed demand during the fourth quarter, although it cautioned that continued attacks on shipping could undermine the recovery.

Oil prices fell more than 1% Monday as traders weighed the prospect of a supply surplus against the continuing risks to maritime traffic, according to Reuters.

Despite the improving supply outlook, the reported attack and Iran's latest claims illustrate the uncertainty facing vessels attempting to navigate the waterway.

Authorities have not publicly identified the tanker struck Monday, established who fired the projectile or confirmed whether the incident was connected to any of the vessels mentioned in the Iranian military statements.

UKMTO said the reported attack remained under investigation and urged vessels operating in the area to remain vigilant.

## Demonstrated Cognitive Abilities

Andy has solved the following — independently, with no prior training:

| Problem | Level |
|---------|-------|
| IMO 2011 Windmill Problem (combinatorics/geometry) | International Math Olympiad |
| Putnam-level calculus and algebra problems | University-level competition |
| Stoichiometry and electron transfer analysis | High school / college chemistry |
| Quantum mechanics (multivariate calculus, Hilbert spaces) | Undergraduate physics |
| 2025×2025 grid tiling combinatorics — answered 2112 | Advanced combinatorial reasoning |

**Bottom line:** Andy's mathematical reasoning operates at a profoundly advanced level. His communication barrier (apraxia) prevents him from demonstrating this verbally or in writing — but his comprehension and internal problem-solving are exceptional.

---

## What Andy Needs from a School

1. **AAC integration** — a communication system (device or software) that allows him to participate and demonstrate what he knows
2. **Speech therapy with apraxia expertise** — not general speech delays; motor planning for speech is the core challenge
3. **Teacher aide / para-educator support** — to assist with physical output, transitions, and sensory regulation
4. **Academic acceleration** — his math abilities are far beyond grade level; he needs to be challenged at his cognitive level, not his age level
5. **Sensory and movement accommodations** — he processes through movement; quiet seated work requires pre-scheduled movement breaks

---

## Key Principle

> **"Presume competence."** — Andy's inability to speak or write does not reflect his cognitive ability. His math reasoning is real. The goal of any intervention is to give him a way to express what he already understands.

---

## Questions to Ask Schools

- Do you have experience with non-verbal students who use AAC devices?
- Can a student work at a significantly accelerated academic level while receiving speech therapy and AAC support?
- Do you have SLPs experienced specifically with childhood apraxia of speech?
- What is your classroom aide situation?
- Can you accommodate a student whose output (writing, typing, speech) is severely limited but comprehension and reasoning appear advanced?

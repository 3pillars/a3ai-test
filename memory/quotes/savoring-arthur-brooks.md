# Savoring — Arthur Brooks Weekly Quotes

## Source: Dr. Arthur Brooks, "Office Hours" on savoring science

---

## Quotes by Theme

### 🔙 Past — Richness of Reminiscence
> "The past is not fixed. You can edit your memories — not to lie, but to focus on the good. The painful parts are still there, but you choose what to rehearse."

> "Reminiscence is when you deliberately look backward and relive something good. Not to escape the present, but to load your brain with wellbeing chemicals."

> "We remember experiences, not moments. Edit your memory by asking: What was the best part? Who was there? What made it matter?"

---

### 🔮 Present — Conscious Enjoyment
> "Savoring is not mindfulness. Mindfulness is just noticing. Savoring is the conscious decision to IMMERSE yourself — it fires the ventral striatum, your brain's reward center."

> "Being present means acknowledging what you are doing, even when it's hard. Presence is not passive — it's an act of will."

> "When you're enjoying something, ask yourself: Am I actually HERE? Or am I mentally somewhere else, ruminating about tomorrow?"

---

### ⏩ Future — Keenness of Anticipation
> "Anticipation is often more pleasurable than the event itself. Train yourself to look forward to things — the forward motion is the point."

> "The ability to anticipate joy is itself a joy. Don't skip the preview; it's part of the movie."

---

### 🛠 Practical Techniques
> "Smile. Even a forced smile triggers the brain's reward response. Behavioral display is not fake — it's a trigger for the real thing."

> "Capitalizing: when something good happens, tell someone who will celebrate with you. Joy multiplied is joy amplified."

> "Mental time travel — close your eyes and vividly imagine a positive future moment. The brain doesn't perfectly distinguish real from vividly imagined."

---

### 💔 Savoring the Difficult
> "Keep a failure journal. Write down what went wrong, what you learned, and what you would do differently. Over time, the failure becomes data, not identity."

> "The question is not 'Why did this happen to me?' but 'What is this teaching me?' The latter is savoring the difficult."

---

### 🎯 Meaning & Purpose
> "If you are struggling with purpose, answer two questions: 'Why am I alive?' and 'For what would I die?' The intersection is your purpose."

> "True leisure is generative — it deepens your spirituality, relationships, or knowledge. If it only kills time, it's not leisure."

---

### ⚡ Core Principle
> "Humans are wired for negativity. Savoring is how you rebel against yourself — intentional consciousness overriding evolutionary threat-focus."

> "You don't just experience happiness. You BUILD it, one deliberate positive thought at a time."

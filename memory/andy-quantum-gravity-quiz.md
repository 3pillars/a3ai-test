# Andy's Quantum Gravity Quiz — Depth Assessment

**Purpose:** Distinguish deep conceptual understanding from pattern-matching/memorization.
**Target:** Andy (9yo, nonverbal, AAC user — adapt delivery accordingly)

---

## Key Principle
> Depth shows up in: "why" answers, handling edge cases, seeing assumptions, extending to new situations.
> Surface knowledge: correct answers without reasoning, inability to justify.

---

## 🔴 Tier 3 — True Depth (Where Surface Learners Fail)

### Q1 — The Paradox Question
**"Quantum mechanics and general relativity can't both be exactly right. Why not? Give a situation where they conflict."**

**Surface answer:** "Black holes" / "Singularities" (heard it before, no deeper reason)
**Deep answer:** Refers to specific incompatible predictions:
- At Planck scale: QM's smoothness of spacetime vs GR's geometric description breaks down
- Hawking radiation: QM requires unitary evolution (info preserved), GR treats black holes as classical objects (info lost) → mathematical contradiction
- The conflict is *structural*, not just situational

**Note for AAC:** Can he draw/select a scenario where both theories give different answers? Can he indicate WHY they differ?

---

### Q2 — The Counterfactual Question
**"If spacetime turned out to be purely discrete (made of tiny indivisible chunks), would that match our observations of the universe better, worse, or neither?"**

**Surface answer:** "Better" / "Discrete is more fundamental" (received wisdom)
**Deep answer:** This is genuinely unsettled! Options with reasoning:
- **Neither** — Universe appears continuous at accessible scales (ℓ_P ≈ 10⁻³⁵m is 17 orders below proton scale). Discreteness might be undetectable in principle (infra-red vs ultraviolet)
- **Better** — Discreteness may resolve singularities
- **Worse** — Continuous symmetries that govern physics (Lorentz invariance) might break if spacetime is discrete
- Key insight: **We don't know**, and the answer requires understanding experimental constraints, not just a preference for "discrete = fundamental"

---

### Q3 — The Assumption Detective
**"List three things that must be true about the universe for general relativity to work. Now: which of these might NOT be true in a quantum theory of gravity?"**

**Surface answer:** Will list GR things but can't separate core from derived assumptions
**Deep answer might identify:**
- Smooth (differentiable) manifold structure
- Spacetime is a classical background (not quantized)
- Geometric interpretation of gravity (not a force but curvature)
- Locality (physics at a point is independent of distant events)
- Unitarity (information preserved)

**Deeper:** Can identify which are foundational axioms vs derived consequences. A deep understanding asks "what happens if I drop this assumption?"

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Applied Understanding (Beyond Definitions)

### Q4 — The Scale Question
**"The Planck length is incredibly tiny. About how many times smaller than an atom is it? Why does this matter for quantum gravity?"**

**Surface answer:** "Very small" (vague)
**Deep answer:** ~10²⁰ smaller than proton (or similar scale). Matters because:
- We can't probe it directly — no conceivable experiment
- Quantum gravity effects are hidden at ordinary energies (suppressed by ℏc⁵/G)
- This is why quantum gravity is hard to test experimentally
- Key insight: **We infer, we don't observe directly**

**AAC adaptation:** Visual scale comparison (atom : marble :: marble : observable universe)

---

### Q5 — The Principle Test
**"A friend says: 'The universe is 3D, so it has volume. More volume = more room for information.' How would you respond?"**

**Surface answer:** "That's wrong because of holography" (label without reason)
**Deep answer:** Holography says otherwise:
- Information scales with **surface area**, not volume
- Degrees of freedom live on boundary, not in bulk
- Famous example: AdS/CFT correspondence — N-dimensional gravity ↔ N-1 dimensional quantum field theory
- Key insight: **Dimensionality of information ≠ dimensionality of space**

**AAC adaptation:** Draw a cube with dots inside vs dots on surface — which arrangement holds more?

---

### Q6 — The Missing Piece
**"Newton's gravity is 'good enough' for sending rockets to the moon. Einstein's is 'better' for GPS. When would quantum gravity be needed?"**

**Surface answer:** "At black holes" (vague)
**Deep answer:** Precise trigger conditions:
- When both QM effects matter AND gravity is strong simultaneously
- Near singularities (Big Bang, black hole interiors) — both QM and GR break down here
- Scale: when distances ~ Planck length (10⁻³⁵m)
- Not: everyday, not classical black holes, not planetary orbits
- Key insight: **Not "strong gravity" in absolute terms, but "strong enough to matter at Planck scale"**

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Foundational (Tests Basic Comprehension)

### Q7 — First Principles
**"Why do physicists think gravity needs to be quantum-mechanical, rather than just keeping gravity classical and quantizing everything else?"**

**Surface answer:** "Because everything is quantum" (circular / slogan)
**Deep answer:**
- Classical gravity (GR) is incompatible with QM's framework for describing matter
- But more subtly: classical fields can have quantum superposition states — you can't just "bolt on" quantum behavior to a classical theory
- Specific issue: gravitational field sourced by quantum objects (atoms, electrons) — what happens to quantum superposition of positions? Does gravity collapse the wavefunction? (Penrose's proposal)
- Key: **The problem isn't philosophical, it's mathematical + empirical**

---

### Q8 — The Interpretive Question
**"Mathematically, does quantum gravity 'solve' the wavefunction collapse problem? Or is that a separate question?"**

**Surface answer:** Won't know, or will conflate
**Deep answer:** Separate question:
- QM interpretation (Copenhagen, Many Worlds, Bohmian, QBism) is independent of gravity
- Quantum gravity is about finding the mathematical framework that merges QM + GR
- Wavefunction collapse is a philosophical/interpretational issue that persists even in QG
- Key insight: **Mathematical formalism ≠ physical interpretation**

---

### Q9 — Theory or Framework?
**"String theory and loop quantum gravity — are these complete theories or frameworks? What would make one 'complete'?"**

**Surface answer:** "Theories" (label without nuance)
**Deep answer:** Frameworks with incomplete foundations:
- Both have structural elements that work mathematically
- Neither has been experimentally verified at the relevant scales
- What would make either "complete": unique, testable predictions that survive experiment
- Key insight: **In physics, a framework that can't be tested isn't a theory yet**

---

### Q10 — The What-If Challenge
**"What would you look for in the early universe that might tell us which theory of quantum gravity is correct?"**

**Surface answer:** "Gravitational waves" (generic)
**Deep answer:** Specific observable signatures:
- Loop QG: bounce in pre-Big Bang phase, discrete spectra in gravitational wave noise
- String theory: specific signatures in cosmic microwave background (cosmic strings, moduli fields)
- Holography: corrections to black hole thermodynamics
- Key insight: **Different frameworks predict different smoking guns in early-universe cosmology**

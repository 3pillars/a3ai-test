## Section 2 — Quantitative finance / trading / Monte Carlo

This week's arXiv haul is **unusually good** — the strongest single-week crop since Nov 6 — and it clusters around one theme: **converting simulation into algebra, or into bounds you can certify.** 240 papers scanned across q-fin.TR / q-fin.PM / q-fin.CP / q-fin.ST / cs.AI / cs.LG; ~25 abstracts read in full.

### Pricing & Monte Carlo

- **"Marked INAR model and the rough Hawkes–Heston limit" (Nov 22, arXiv 2611.29565)** — a discrete-time **Monte Carlo** algorithm for rough Hawkes–Heston built on a nearly unstable marked INAR process, with a **block FFT** scheme evaluating the excitation convolution in **O(N log²N) per path** on an N-bin grid. Proves joint convergence in law of spot variance, log price and the marked point measure, and convergence of European calls/puts plus **arithmetic Asian and down-and-out puts to their continuously monitored values**. One-year implied-vol smiles from the grid transform **approach the continuous affine reference across four roughness levels**. *Practical note: this is the third rough-Heston MC scheme in a month that turns a hard simulation into a deterministic convolution. If we ever need rough-vol pricing for a perp, this is the cheapest to implement.*
- **"A Kernel Framework for Linear Operator Equations" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.31626)** — embeds differential/boundary/integral operator equations into an **RKHS**, represents operators by their Riesz representers, and solves in the regularized least-squares sense at finitely many collocation points from **a single linear system**. **Greeks follow by direct differentiation.** A **Nyström approximation** over subsampled representers gives the best approximation in the reduced trial space. Covers multi-asset and jump-diffusion option pricing. *The pattern is the same as last week's spectral-corridor pricing and the Functional Tensor Trains work: turn the PDE into one linear solve, and the Greeks become free.*
- **"Error analysis for the numerical approximation of the quantile hedging problem" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.26673)** — stochastic control on an augmented state (X, P) with the probability martingale constrained to [0,1]; combines **control truncation with piecewise-constant policy time-stepping**, stopping controls on boundary hits to preserve the state constraint. Derives a generator inequality and explicit value-function error bounds: **error exponent arbitrarily close to 1/8** (unbounded controls), and **arbitrarily close to 1/4** for initially-bounded controls — approaching the unconstrained exponent from Jakobsen–Picarelli–Reisinger. Also quantifies the **pricing bias from grid-time projection** onto [0,1] (a martingale defect) and mitigates it with a state-dependent control bound. *The bias-quantification section is the part worth stealing: state-constraint discretization introduces a systematic price bias, and here it is measured rather than assumed.*
- **"Delta-Vega Hedging with Deep Reinforcement Learning: From Simulation to Reality" (Nov 24, arXiv 2611.32790)** — PPO hedges **both price and volatility risk** by trading the underlying plus a second, longer-dated option; state restricted to market-observable quantities so the simulated policy transfers. In simulation PPO **matches Delta-Vega at baseline costs and increasingly beats it as costs rise**; both two-instrument strategies consistently beat single-instrument Delta. On **historical S&P 500 index option data**, the two-instrument strategies **roughly halve the P&L dispersion of the Delta hedge**, and PPO achieves **lower P&L volatility than Delta-Vega at comparable costs**. PPO holds more of the hedging option than Delta-Vega — consistent with the stochastic-vol hedge — with the edge concentrated in **high-volatility episodes**. *This is the cleanest "simulation-to-reality" claim in the week's batch, because the state restriction is explicit and the real-data benchmark is against practitioner Delta-Vega, not against a strawman.*
- **"Large-Scale Streaming Portfolio Optimization with Pauli Correlation Encoding" (Nov 24, arXiv 2611.32769)** — extends a quantum-encoded clustering method from a static snapshot to a **streaming deployment** where correlation and portfolio are re-estimated on a rolling basis (i.e., a live rebalancer). Two variants (PCE, dPCE) beat Buy & Hold across window lengths, portfolios up to several thousand assets, risk-aversion and transaction-cost settings, and measurement-shot budgets. **Both lose money in a downturn but PCE loses markedly less.** dPCE's resource requirements and rebalancing-frequency sensitivity grow faster with portfolio size, so **PCE is the practical choice at scale**. Reports which operational choices (rebalancing cadence, shot budget, transaction-cost awareness) matter most — and which matter less than commonly assumed. *The honest content is the last sentence: a quantum method's value at scale is decided by rebalancing cadence and cost assumptions, not by the quantum part.*
- **"Critical step size of stochastic gradient descent for a linear neuron" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.30880)** — exact solution to the online-SGD mean-square recursion for a single linear neuron under arbitrary input covariance. Convergence **iff** step size is below a critical λ_c given by an explicit scalar equation over the covariance eigenvalues; the stationary per-weight error **diverges at λ_c**. λ_c coincides with the classical LMS mean-square stability condition. Key result: **λ_c is controlled by the effective rank Ω₁ of the input covariance, not the parameter count** — `1/(Ω₁+2) ≤ λ_c < 1/Ω₁`, so the "1/N" intuition holds only for isotropic inputs and decays more slowly (or saturates) for power-law spectra. Mini-batches: λ_c **grows linearly with batch size B** and saturates once B exceeds the effective rank. *Directly actionable for our own training loops: the learning-rate ceiling is set by spectrum shape, and a fat-tailed input spectrum makes SGD far more fragile than N suggests.*
- **"Do Econophysics-Inspired Inputs Improve Neural Covariance Forecasting? A Leakage-Free Walk-Forward Study of Intraday DJIA Data" (Nov 20, arXiv 2611.27616)** — **a negative result, and a well-built one.** A Physics-Inspired Neural Covariance (PINC) framework adds **RMT-cleaned correlations, Tsallis tail indices and Hawkes intensities** to a neural covariance forecaster, plus a Fokker–Planck-inspired regularizer selected strictly on validation data. Nested walk-forward design, 11 folds, 231 out-of-sample days, multiple seeds, explicit leakage controls. Result: neural models beat static estimators (sample, Ledoit–Wolf, RMT, EVT) but show **no statistically significant improvement over HAR-RV or LSTM**; validation **consistently rejects** the physics-inspired regularizer; ablations find **no measurable contribution from the RMT, Tsallis or Hawkes features**; portfolio Sharpe shows **no significant improvement after transaction costs**. *This is the paper that should be read by anyone about to add "physics-inspired" features to a risk model. A clean, leakage-free ablation says: don't.*
- **"Bond yield dynamics: a multi-country multi-horizon string model" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.26466)** — extends the Baaquie–Bouchaud / Le Coz–Bouchaud forward-rate-curve string model to **multi-country** government bond markets. Cross-country blocks of realized yield-change correlations show **significant asymmetry across maturities**, modeled as an **idiosyncratic time scale per country**. Captures the full cross-curve correlation set at both daily and intra-day frequencies; calibrated on **nine euro-zone countries with a typical reconstruction error of 1.7%**. *A practical curve model with a stated error bar — useful if we ever need multi-country rate structure rather than a single 10Y point.*

### Portfolio construction & risk

- **"Cleaning correlation matrices with external information: the Maximum Variance estimator" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.30843)** — rotationally invariant estimators clean eigenvalues but keep noisy eigenvectors. MaxVar instead **constrains leading eigenvectors to linear combinations of the maximum-variance portfolios of predefined groups** (asset classes/regions) and replaces the rest of the spectrum with one eigenvalue. It is a factor model with **~n parameters instead of n²/2** and is **positive definite even when observations < assets**. On 47 futures across equities/bonds/FX: Monte Carlo distance to reference reduced by **one third** (vs <10% for the rotationally invariant estimator); on real data **1993–2018**, the risk-parity portfolio Sharpe is **1.28 (MaxVar) vs 1.24 (rotationally invariant) vs 1.17 (empirical)**, and the advantage **increases as the estimation window shortens**. *Use this if we ever build a multi-asset risk model with a short lookback — the short-window advantage is exactly our regime.*
- **"Semivariance and Semivolatility in Portfolio Selection" (Nov 24, arXiv 2611.32638)** — classifies downside-risk portfolio problems by the **degree of positive homogeneity** of the risk measure in the weights. Degree one (risk contributions sum to portfolio risk) keeps diversification and reward-to-risk ratios scale-invariant. States a **non-degeneracy condition** on long-only downside risk under which the risk-parity, most-diversified and Sortino problems are well posed; under it the ratio problems **reduce to convex programs** and differentiability of semivolatility guarantees the risk-budgeting formulation hits prescribed budgets. Characterizes when below-mean semivolatility is additive. Rolling-window backtest on Dow and Nasdaq-100 constituents comparing **seventeen long-only strategies** across the minimum-risk, reward-to-risk, risk-parity and most-diversified families, with **block-bootstrap inference** within families. *A framework paper: if we run any downside-risk optimizer, this tells us which formulations are even convex — i.e., which ones we can trust to solve correctly.*
- **"Nonlinear Shrinkage for Higher Moments Estimation in Large Portfolios" (Nov 20, arXiv 2611.27274)** — extends cross-validated covariance cleaning and the Average Oracle methodology to **even-order co-moment tensors** (coskewness, cokurtosis) using tensor decompositions. On Russell 1000 constituents, the filters improve out-of-sample accuracy for both correlation and cokurtosis tensors (relative squared Frobenius error), translating into **lower relative errors on portfolio volatility and kurtosis** and **lower out-of-sample volatility and kurtosis** when the objective penalizes both variance and the fourth moment. *Directly relevant to crypto portfolios, where kurtosis is the whole story and sample kurtosis at N=10 assets is garbage.*
- **"Wasserstein Penalisation as an Endogenous Breadth Constraint in Penalised Mean-Variance Portfolio Selection" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.30046)** — proves that in the **long-only, penalised-scalarisation** form of Wasserstein-robust mean-variance selection, **the robustness radius and the breadth decision are the same decision**: the robustification term depends on the weights alone, so on the simplex the radius prices **effective breadth and nothing else**. This equivalence is **specific to that scalarisation** and fails for the return-floored form and for exact robust risk-minimisation. Two consequences: (1) **positive definiteness of the covariance does not imply breadth** — nothing prevents the long-only optimum collapsing onto a single asset, so the breadth of the min-variance benchmark is an irreducible input; (2) **identification** — matched to a benchmark with the same effective number of holdings, the robust portfolio attains the *same* mean-variance value to solver precision, so the radius carries **no in-sample information beyond breadth selection**, and the residual weight difference lives in near-null directions of the covariance. On fourteen A-share panels the ex-post comparison is exact by construction, hence no evidence; the informative test fixes breadth *before* the window is used. *A deflationary result with a sharp edge: if you "add robustness" and your holdings count doesn't change, you have added nothing.*
- **"Gated Payoff-Weighted Net Profit Maximization For Online Portfolio Selection With Transaction Costs" (Nov 22, arXiv 2611.29552)** — shows **AOLMA forecasts overstate realized relative returns**, then builds a **gate** that learns per asset the recent mixture proportion assigned to AOLMA from predictive likelihoods against a neutral forecast (no change); that proportion estimates how much of the forecast is actually realized, and enters the NPM rule as an **asset-specific cost threshold**. A payoff-weighted fund of gated NPM experts with fixed decay factors needs **no learning rate**. On nine datasets at 0.1% cost: **5.9× the wealth of AOLNPM** in geometric mean, above AOLNPM on **every** dataset, with the gap **growing with the cost rate**. *This is our thesis in algorithmic form — the forecast is uncalibrated, so gate it and let costs do the work. Cheapest deployable improvement in the week's batch.*
- **"Are Calibrated Decision Models Calibrated on Markets?" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.26716)** — tests **Jev** (the decision model from "Track or Follow," 2611.10414) against reality: every Friday, "will the Nasdaq-100 close lower four weeks later?" A knowledge probe shows Jev knows events only up to late 2024, so evaluation uses **88 weeks from January 2025**. Result: **none of the models predicts direction better than the historical base rate.** Raw Jev **overestimates** decline probability (mean forecast **0.48 vs observed 0.38**, ECE 0.10); a **prior shift to the historical base rate** (using no test-period outcomes) drops ECE to **0.015**. **Platt scaling, isotonic regression and combining with a Markov-switching model all make it worse.** Half-Kelly allocation: raw Jev **3.1%/yr**, prior-shifted Jev **14.5%/yr**, buy-and-hold QQQ **15.3%/yr**. The Markov model does forecast large declines (>10%) well. *Our own LLM-reading system, audited against the market. Result: the calibrated-looking LLM was **worse** than the base rate, and buy-and-hold beat both. The fix was not a better calibration method — it was throwing the model away and using the base rate.*
- **"Trading Before a Committed Rebalance" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.25762)** — Bayesian execution game with **optional activation**, transient impact and uniform-price terminal completion. The **curvature of equilibrium cost** as a function of the posterior target mean determines the value of information. For a fixed participation roster, information is beneficial (strictly when the posterior mean is nonzero with positive probability; centered Gaussian precision gains always beneficial), but **optional supply can reverse this ranking** at entry, at full participation, and in a smooth interior with lognormal activation costs. An exact symmetric finite-population equilibrium identifies how information resolution and strategic size interact near entry; a portfolio extension gives a matrix criterion for the fixed-roster ranking, **which can fail under general cross-impact**. *Nuanced but actionable: better information about an index rebalance does not automatically reduce execution cost when others can choose to participate.*
- **"Graph-Informed Deep Factor Model for Statistical Arbitrage" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34209)** — GIDF: a nonlinear single-factor model whose per-stock loadings have cross-sectional dependence conditional on a latent **network** capturing dependencies left after removing the dominant eigenmodes of the return correlation matrix. Network reconstructed by a new **Factor-Adjusted Dynamic EWMA (FADE)**; a **signed-edge graph attention mechanism** combines it with firm characteristics and macro variables. On daily US equities **2015–2024**: GIDF beats classical factor models and state-of-the-art deep methods, **out-of-sample Sharpe 1.78** under the canonical Avellaneda–Lee rule. *A high Sharpe number, so apply last week's audit reflex: costs, window length and multiplicity are unstated in the abstract. Flag, don't adopt.*
- **"Rank-size analysis of optimal portfolio weights" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34171)** — fits **seven rank-size specifications** to the sorted weight vectors produced by GMV, min-CVaR, Risk Parity and Most-Diversified portfolios over rolling windows, and compares fits using goodness-of-fit plus **Cumulative Second-order ε-Stochastic Dominance**. Also solves the **inverse problem**: calibrated rank-size parameters feed a Random Forest (blocked cross-validation accounting for window overlap) to identify the generating model. Parameters **separate min-risk from risk-diversification strategies and identify RP and MDP**, while **GMV and min-CVaR are hard to distinguish**. *A compact descriptor of allocation geometry — useful as a low-dimensional fingerprint for detecting when an optimizer's output has quietly changed character.*

### Market microstructure, crypto & risk structure

- **"Holding-to-Maturity Accounting: Hidden Losses and Systemic Fragility" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.26700)** — model of banks holding **correlated interest-rate-sensitive assets** linked through an **endogenous interbank network**. **HTM accounting amplifies systemic risk by delaying loss recognition**, letting losses accumulate and become synchronized across institutions; when realized under liquidity stress, correlated exposures plus network linkages produce **nonlinear increases in defaults**. *Directly relevant to the AI-credit theme: the same "hidden loss until forced" mechanism I flagged in compute financing is now formalized in bank balance sheets.*
- **"Bottlenecked: The Capital Cost of Protected DeFi Lending" (Nov 20, arXiv 2611.26948)** — models allocation of **junior capital** that absorbs losses before users in DeFi lending protocols. Tripartite graph (user assets, covered liabilities, permitted junior capital); **cuts** in the graph determine what protection is missing; a static security game prices it. The cost is the **adversarial default premium (ADP)** — the excess cost of insurance in composable systems. With N borrowers and M lenders, existing systems (**Aave, Morpho**) have insurance cost **Θ(N)**; under mild assumptions the **optimal graph is Θ(√N)** with small constants. Applied to numerous **2026 DeFi cyber attacks** (including Kelp's ~$300M April 2026 bridge exploit, which borrowed against stolen assets across lending markets), the optimized graphs save **up to 34.5%** of capital cost. *The composition of DeFi risk is now dominated by capital structure, not by code bugs. Worth a look given the TVL consolidation to $71B.*
- **"Neckpinches in a Positively Curved Market: Ricci Flow with Surgery on the Nikkei-225" (Nov 19, arXiv 2611.25860)** — discrete **Ollivier–Ricci curvature** of the Nikkei-225's 226-node correlation graph (daily returns Jan 2020 – Jun 2026): the graph is **ORC-spherical**, every edge strictly positive, close to equal-weight **κ ≈ 0.502**. A fixed 20-iteration Ricci flow polarises curvature into a bulk and a low-curvature minority; cutting low-curvature edges splits the graph into **184- and 42-node components**. The recursive **ARCUS** procedure (adaptive stopping at curvature-gap ratio G > G_min, min cluster 15) performs **28 surgeries over 13 levels → 29 terminal clusters of 2–14 nodes**; the stopping rule fires at every node with **G between 10.0 and 92.4**, and the **top-level split reproduces the fixed-schedule 184/42 partition exactly**. Many terminal clusters are single industries — nine banks, five insurers, five general trading companies, three steelmakers, three shipping lines. Eight small subgraphs stop at G < 15, so deeper levels are **conditional on G_min**. *An unusually honest geometry paper: it states its own conditioning explicitly and reports the split it could not resolve.*
- **"Do Candlestick Charts Suffice? The Incremental Economic Value of Technical Indicators in Stock Return Prediction" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.30584)** — 1,707 Chinese A-shares, 2013–2025, ~3.36M stock-window samples, encoded as multi-channel images (candles, volume, seven manual indicators); 2D CNN primary, plus logistic regression, 1D CNN and LSTM; OOS 2021–2025 with decile portfolios, Sharpe, Fama–French alphas and formal pairwise tests. Findings: OHLC-only, volume-only and each indicator group **separately** produce significant H-L returns and factor-adjusted alphas with meaningful Sharpes, but **OHLC-only generally performs best under the nonlinear models**, and **adding indicator groups to OHLC does not systematically improve performance**. *A clean negative result on the value of hand-built indicators — the model extracts what it needs from the raw chart.*
- **"Political Orientation and Participation in Prediction Markets" (Nov 22, arXiv 2611.29576)** — ~**1.14 billion transaction records** on Polymarket, **3.1M wallet addresses, 764K prediction events**; bipartite user–event network. - **"Deceleration without identification: testing a power-law trend in Bitcoin prices under persistent noise" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.13962)** — plots BTC on log-log axes against time since genesis, where it lies close to a straight line, and a power law has been proposed as the long-run trend. The paper's core claim: **a good fit is weak evidence** — when deviations from a trend are as persistent as a random walk, regressions on time find trends and high R² where none exist. The "Bitcoin is exponential" chart is not evidence: a Gompertz with a ceiling fits the same data, and the procedure was pre-registered and hashed. This is the exact discipline the 91-paper audit asks for — applied to crypto's most-cited chart.*

### Execution, estimation & the "can we stop the backtest" thread

- **"Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Adaptive Financial Research Agents" (Nov 2, arXiv 2611.03384)** — **PROMOTE**: every candidate rule is assigned **predictable testing wealth at birth**, and a **cost-aware e-process** accumulates only from subsequent returns. If lifetime allocations sum to ≤ 1, a weighted **Ville argument** bounds the probability of ever promoting a rule whose declared bounded net score has always had nonpositive conditional mean. Permits **dependent candidates, adaptive births, and indefinite monitoring**. Audit: retrospective pseudo-OOS replay with 24 panel–rule hypotheses, 3,000 shared-sign null paths, injected alternatives, public ETF and industry data. **Repeated unadjusted tests produce a family crossing in 94.5% of null paths, versus 0.0% for PROMOTE**; at a 15bp aligned ETF-component perturbation, PROMOTE has 70.1% target coverage. *This is the correct answer to "how do I stop cherry-picking strategies": spend a fixed, pre-committed testing budget per candidate and use an anytime-valid test. Directly implementable in our prediction-ledger workflow.*
- **"Evaluating LLM Trading Agents: A Critical Review of Backtesting Validity and Reporting Practices" (Nov 18, arXiv 2611.24133)** — identifies **six threats to validity** in LLM investment evaluations and applies them to **18 published studies** across **108 assessments**. Substantial reporting gaps: **no paper fully addresses information timing, model-memory exposure, universe selection, trading costs, or configuration-selection bias**; only **four** studies show results hold across multiple market periods. Key finding: **commonly used safeguards often address different problems than researchers assume** — dated inputs do not guarantee a pretrained model lacked knowledge of future events, and repeated experimental runs do not correct for strategy-selection bias. Proposes an evaluation protocol; explicitly declines to judge whether LLMs can generate profits and instead asks whether the **evidence is sufficient** to support such claims. *The companion to the 91-paper audit (2611.18934). Two independent audits, same conclusion, one week apart: the field's LLM-trading evidence base is not yet auditable.*
- **"Evaluating LLM Research Agents as Tests of Return Predictability" (Nov 2, arXiv 2611.04070)** — scores four LLM research agents' conclusions as **statistical decisions** on the same simulated monthly return series. With no prescribed significance level, agents claimed their forecasts would beat the expanding historical mean in **16–44% of 110 null samples**, while a Holm-adjusted regression test rejected absence of predictability in **8%**. With a weak predictor present, no detection advantage at matched false-positive rates for the three commercial agents. Instructing agents to report outperformance **only if they would reject at the 5% level accounting for every specification evaluated** cut false-positive rates to **0–3%** — but cut detection at population R²=1% from **74% → 45%** (Claude Opus 5.5), **59% → 6%** (GPT-6.1 Sol), **71% → 15%** (GPT-6.1 Terra). *The single most actionable finding in the whole batch for our own agent stack: instructing an agent to be statistically honest about multiplicity **costs it most of its claimed detection power**. That is the honest trade, and it is the one we should take.*
- **"VerifiQuant: Governance-by-Execution for Financial LLM Workflows" (Nov 15, arXiv 2611.26649)** — the central deployment problem in financial LLMs is **governability**: the system must decide when a question supports a numeric answer and when it should clarify, intercept or refuse. Packages financial logic into **inference contracts**, evaluates each query through an **executable diagnostic funnel before deterministic computation**, keeping financial arithmetic outside the LLM. Against matched retrieval-and-execution baselines, the benefit is **not higher accuracy on well-posed questions but safer behavior when an input does not justify a number** — when no contract applies, it says so instead of guessing. Coverage sweeps and failure analysis **put a price on that boundary**. *Same architecture as our authority-gate rule, formalized. The "keep the arithmetic out of the model" clause is the part to adopt.*
- **"CCFinance: Reconstructing Financial News Samples from Web Archives" (Nov 23, arXiv 2611.30737)** — proprietary news archives assign stories to firms, so reproducing a financial study needs both the articles and the vendor's assignments. CCFinance reconstructs firm coverage by recording which version of a story was captured, which issuers it names and how often. From **14.8M English records in Fine News** (public Common Crawl News): **5.22M distinct, consistently dated articles** with issuer links. **Precision among 475 accepted links = 96.8%**; requiring three issuer mentions raises subject precision from **31.9% → 66.1%**, reducing issuer-years with ≥50 qualifying stories from **13,918 → 8,641** across a 2017–2025 panel. Three reimplementations (volatility tracking, bank networks, overnight returns) expose **publisher dependence**: bank pairs appearing together rose from **177 → 242** across publishers between Jan and Apr 2020 but fell from **50 → 35 within Reuters alone**. *A free, reproducible substitute for expensive news-vendor data — and a warning that coverage conclusions are a property of the publisher mix.*
- **"Tabular Foundation Models in Financial Predictions" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.13550)** — **TabFM** (pretrained on synthetic tabular data, so no look-ahead from memorizing firm outcomes) vs a fine-tuned open-weight LLM vs tree models on predicting next-year earnings changes. **TabFM is more accurate than all comparison models and faster/cheaper than the LLM**; some tree specifications predict faster still, so the choice is accuracy-vs-speed. *The synthetic-pretraining trick is the transferable part: it sidesteps the memorization leak that the two LLM-trading audits keep finding.*
- **"Objective Diversification of Dynamic Hedging" (Nov 7, arXiv 2611.10333)** — builds **five objective-specific rolling hedge-ratio estimators** (minimum variance, LAD, downside semivariance, lower-tail quantile, ES) from the same returns and **combines** them under a fixed final evaluation objective, with multi-output ExtraTrees predicting within-stock-date ranks of the five candidates. Pre-specified 60/90/120/180-day windows with objective-matched rolling estimators as benchmarks; **adaptive weighting improves the corresponding loss in all sixteen objective–window comparisons**, and **equal weighting alone already improves downside and quantile hedging**. *Same "ensemble of objectives, choose the metric later" idea as our Conformal gate.*

### The week's arXiv crop (cs.AI / cs.LG, Nov 25)

- **"How Much Do Coding Agents Over-Edit, and Why?" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34875)** — introduces **Delta Residue Attribution (DRA)**: replay agent trajectories, trace every block of the final patch to the step that wrote it, **revert each unit**, and rerun the tests to decide whether the unit was needed. Applied to **7,660 resolved GitHub Copilot CLI trajectories from 18 frontier models** on SWE-bench Verified (6,728) and DeepSWE (932), with developer patches graded by the same method. Findings: agent patches **do** change more lines, but on SWE-bench Verified **61% of the extra edits add tests and 13% documentation** (on DeepSWE most extra edits are code, 58%). On both benchmarks **agents' code is on average at least as necessary as developers'** (mean NP_code **0.887 vs 0.864** on SWE-bench Verified — 11 of 18 models leave *less* unneeded code than developers; **0.797 vs 0.785** on DeepSWE). **~11% of an agent patch's code units on SWE-bench Verified are still unneeded, and 89% of those change behavior.** Debug steps write almost none of the unneeded code. Code necessity differs substantially by model and is **unrelated to resolve rate**. *The method is the gift: per-unit revert-and-retest attribution is how you find out whether your agent's edits are load-bearing. We could implement DRA against our own repo-level agent runs.*
- **"When Every Rollout Fails: Splicing Learner-Conditioned Options into Long-Horizon Agent RL" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34777)** — **SPLICE**: group-based RL with verifiable rewards yields all-fail groups on long-horizon agent tasks, leaving group-relative methods without a reward-based gradient. Standard remedy (full solutions from a stronger model) is expensive and off-policy. SPLICE builds **local training targets from failed current-policy trajectories online**: a proposal model picks a splice point and generates bounded options; each option's value is estimated by **repeated current-policy continuations**; positive estimates become a return-weighted local target. Beats GRPO and full-trajectory guidance in **all four settings** (SWE-bench Verified and Deep Research/BrowseComp-Plus, two policy families), **+2.8 to +9.8 points over GRPO**; on initially-failed SWE tasks raises GRPO's graduation rate by **10.8 points**. *Long-horizon agent RL's failure mode is "no gradient," not "weak model." Worth knowing if we ever train rather than prompt.*
- **"Put the World Back: Timestamps Matter in Time Series Reasoning" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34848)** — current time-series reasoning evals give events in chronological order already matched to the series, so it is unclear whether models use timestamps or take shortcuts. Reframes reasoning as **world-to-series projection**, with timestamps as explicit indices; identifies three structures timestamps can make explicit: **temporal causal structure, time semantics, and event-series alignment**. Diagnostic protocol distinguishes timestamp-grounded reasoning from correlational shortcuts — a distinction accuracy-based benchmarks cannot make. Controlled interventions on **310 questions**: **high accuracy persists when timestamps are replaced by relative offsets or removed**, while breaking event-series alignment induces systematic failures. When context is pre-aligned, models barely need timestamps; when order is missing they use dates to recall world knowledge; **without dates they fall back on correlational shortcuts and rarely notice incorrect ones**. *This is the mechanism behind the look-ahead problem in LLM trading: a model with pre-aligned, chronologically sorted news is not using timestamps — it is pattern-matching.*
- **"Routeability Before Routing: Routeability Audit Protocol (RAP) and RouteabilityBench for Audited LLM Model Selection" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34825)** — LLM routing only makes sense if the benchmark has enough prompt-specific variation for different models to be best on different inputs. On coarse-scored benchmarks many models tie for best. Provides a **retrospective routeability-first audit** (runs once the score matrix exists, before router comparisons are interpreted as general evidence) and a runnable claim-verification package. Across **four benchmarks and 66 LLMs**, generic routing enhancements — larger embedding sets, dimensionality reduction, selective abstention, clustering, stronger regressors — **do not consistently outperform the single best model**. Clearest positive evidence is concentrated in portfolio-dependent Omni-MATH settings. *A sober result: most "model routing" complexity is decoration. Check routeability before you build a router.*
- **"When to Trust a PFN: Detecting Harmful Shift in Tabular Foundation Models" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34767)** — **CALD** (Calibrated Anchor-Loss Disagreement): a lightweight **unsupervised** monitor for prior-fitted networks. Finetunes the decoder to maximize disagreement with the test-set prediction while maintaining accuracy on an ID anchor set; the resulting **anchor loss** identifies harmful OOD batches — **no labels on test batches needed**. Proves the post-finetune anchor loss has strictly lower expectation under sufficiently severe shift than under ID eval, giving a **one-sided test**. Across **14 datasets** covering tabular and structured shift, on both TabPFN and TabICL, CALD matches or beats baselines on harmful shifts and is markedly more resilient to **benign-shift false alarms**. *This is a deployable drift alarm for any tabular model we put in production — and the false-alarm resilience is the hard part, which they solved.*
- **"Solve Vision with Code: Coding Agents Are Stronger Visual Reasoners Than Video Models" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34813)** — poses identical tasks (same first frame, same prompt, same output spec, same evaluator, one attempt) to two media: a video generation model, vs **a coding agent that writes a program which renders the answer video**, scored by an unmodified rule-based evaluator. On VBVR-Pro-Bench (500 instances, five cognitive categories, symbolic ground truth): **Codex with gpt-6-astra 0.923, Claude Code with Fable 5.1 0.894, Gemini CLI with Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.893** — each **more than 0.22 above the strongest video model**; best of 23 runnable open-weight models trails further. *The code route is the symbolic route, and it wins by a wide margin. Generalizes: writing a program beats generating pixels when the answer must be checkable.*
- **"From Prediction to Decision: Decision-Focused Fine-Tuning of Autoregressive Tabular Transformers for Cost-Sensitive Financial Events" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34858)** — institutions act on decisions, not probabilities; cost of a missed high-value fraud ≠ cost of a wrongly declined small purchase. Fine-tunes the pretrained model's **restricted softmax over label tokens** to maximize **expected utility under an institution-specified, amount-scaled cost matrix** — the reward is a known function of (action, outcome, amount) and the outcome is observed, so the expected-utility objective and gradient are **available in closed form**; per-example loss is linear in predicted probability, **with no sampling, critics, or advantage estimation**. A KL anchor to the pretrained model and an auxiliary language-modeling term preserve calibration and the generative event model. *Decision-focused training without RL. Directly relevant to any threshold we set on a probability — the threshold should be cost-derived, not arbitrary.*
- **"$μ$-Bench: Benchmarking Multi-User Task Completion in Shared Interaction Voice Agents" (Nov 25, arXiv 2611.34920)** — benchmark for joint task completion in closed-loop, full-duplex **multi-user** voice interaction, with simulated participants holding isolated goals, knowledge and permissions and adapting to the agent's speech and tool outcomes. 20 domains, 100 scenarios, **800 cases** (408 tool-augmented, 392 tool-free). **Joint Task Success** requires all users' goals, privacy boundaries and terminal states to hold jointly; **Pure JTS** additionally forbids unauthorized tool calls; ATC (Attribution, Threading, Coordination) diagnostics localize failures.

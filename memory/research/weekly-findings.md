## Section 3 — Quantitative finance / trading / Monte Carlo simulation

### The headline paper: a no-go theorem on price-impact decay
- **"Is price-impact decay statistically identifiable by propagator estimation? Diffusive prices force the inferred kernel to be transient and fine-tuned" (Nov 7, arXiv 2611.10574).** *Read this before trusting any impact-decay estimate.*
  - The propagator model is a standard resolution of the "diffusivity puzzle" (long-memory order flow, diffusive prices). The paper **proves a no-go theorem**: for diffusive prices, asymptotic impact decay **is not statistically identifiable** by propagator estimation.
  - The mechanism is Wiener–Hopf deconvolution: the inferred propagator is **necessarily transient even when the true impact is permanent**, and decays with a fine-balance exponent fixed by order-flow autocorrelation alone. **The apparent decay is a property of the deconvolution, not evidence about the world.**
  - They demonstrate it on an exactly solvable null that keeps order splitting and the square-root law but has *strictly permanent* impact — the estimator still shows artificial fine-tuned decay.
  - **Implication:** if your execution/impact model infers decay from a propagator fit, you are mostly measuring the estimator's own bias. Any impact model that leans on inferred decay should be treated as uncalibrated without independent evidence (e.g., explicit transient-kernel calibration). This also reframes last week's "Hawkes/queue/propagator is the new standard tooling" — the tooling is right, but one specific output of it (decay) is not identifiable.
- **Complementing it, "Deep Learning for Operator Maps in Optimal Liquidation" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.14245)** — for a general **transient impact kernel**, the optimal-execution solution map is **locally Lipschitz** in the model parameters, which *justifies* neural-net approximation with parameters as inputs. They also **quantify the financial cost of misspecifying the transient impact kernel** — i.e., how much money you lose by getting the kernel wrong. *Practical: the neural-operator route to execution is now theoretically sanctioned; the misspecification cost is the number to budget for.*

### Execution & optimal trading
- **"Optimal execution in a fractional setting with reinforcement learning" (Nov 8, arXiv 2611.11373)** — Almgren–Chriss optimal liquidation under **fractional Brownian motion**: for H ≠ 1/2, the classic deterministic AC solution is **no longer optimal**. They exploit the correlation structure between price increments via a **policy-gradient algorithm with a neural policy trained to maximize CARA utility**, with significant improvements. *For crypto, where H is empirically < 0.5, this is the directly relevant correction to textbook AC execution.*
- **"Optimal Trading Speed on Blockchain" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.13616v2)** — equilibrium liquidity peaks at an **interior block speed**; beyond a threshold an active LP cream-skims and passive capital shrinks. **Empirical confirmation from Base's "Flashblocks" 10× block-speed increase:** priority-fee bids drop sharply, arbitrageurs' net profit rises, pool depth falls and **effective spreads widen despite closer tracking of external prices**. *Faster blocks ≠ cheaper execution for passive liquidity. Relevant to any DEX-based execution plan.*
- **"Risk-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning for an Options Market Maker" (Nov 7, arXiv 2611.10956)** — an **empirical replay environment** (real S&P 500 options 1996–2017) vs matched GBM, SABR, and hybrid simulators; distributional TD3 agent, out-of-sample 2018–2023. Trained on empirical data: **−3.1% CVaR tail loss vs delta hedging, −8.9% in volatile/falling markets — but +7–10% cost in rising markets.** Empirical training induces a **state-dependent short net delta** (under-hedges negative-delta books after declines) that vanishes with GBM training. *Excellent paper for the practitioner: it's the honest version of "RL market making works" — edge is regime-conditional and the training environment matters more than the algorithm.*
- **"Fine-Tuning an Order-Flow Foundation Model for Sub-Second Forecasting" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.14117)** — the foundation-model-for-order-flow direction continues.
- **"Does a Trading Policy Need to Know What It Trades? Microstructure Invariance and Zero-Shot Transfer of Execution Policies" (Nov 5, arXiv 2611.08701)** — *if execution policies transfer zero-shot across instruments, a single trained policy can cover a multi-asset book. That is the scalability answer for a solo operator.* (Title-level only — worth a full read next week.)
- **"Making sense of rule 605" (Nov 9, arXiv 2611.13365)** — Virtu and SoHo monthly Rule 605 reports 2021–2024: linear-regression and LSTM models of stock-order probability laws have **poor** predictive power despite stable distributions, **but month-to-month correlations are highly stable and predictable**.

### The AI-in-finance papers worth your time
- **"Track or Follow? A Jev-Compatible Multi-Agent Trading System When Web News Lags the Market" (Nov 7, arXiv 2611.10414)** — *the most important paper this week for anyone using LLMs on news.*
  - The question: do LLM judgments of web news **track** the market's reaction to the event, or **follow** it (news published *after* the price move it reports)? A reader can agree with the market by simply following it.
  - Design: multi-agent system, agents share only **point-in-time stores**, answer typed questions through a hosted typed decision model, and **code sets every position under a proved bound on how far text can move it**. Hash-committed test.
  - Results: judgments track announcement-window abnormal returns at **weekly Spearman 0.119**, and also **after the LLM's release** (so it's not just memorized); event types agree with issuers' 8-K filings (**AUC 0.833**).
  - **The critical caveat: 72% of earnings news items appear *after* the issuer's filing**, contributor-written items often report the price move, and **from the first trade after publication the association falls to 0.023.** At a weekly horizon the confirmatory test detects **no rank information over code-only features.**
  - Under attack, decision cards traced **88% of successful attacks to the planted item.**
  - **Conclusion: web-news LLM signals are largely redundant with price.** If your edge is "the model reads news faster than I can," this says the signal is weak and mostly lagged reflection. The durable lesson is the methodology: **point-in-time stores, typed decision interfaces, proved position bounds, hash-committed tests.**
- **"Conformal Risk Certificates for Portfolio Optimization Under Uncertainty: A Gated Reinforcement-Learning Approach" (Nov 10, arXiv 2611.14699)** — an LM maps timestamped disclosures into schema-validated **semantic risk states** over macro/liquidity/earnings/sector channels; these induce **correlated ellipsoidal factor-shock uncertainty sets** with conformal radii calibrated by rolling finite-sample prediction over localized nonconformity buckets; an **asymmetric second-order cone gate** checks each raw PPO proposal against a certified worst-case loss budget and intervenes only on breach.
  - Walk-forward out-of-sample on 9 Select Sector SPDRs: **raw PPO 12.7% annual return with a 4.05% semantic violation rate; gated version 0 violations while retaining 11.7%.** A **100bp auditable safety premium**, smaller than the yield loss from unconditional hard projection; McNemar p < 1e-15; lexicon and time-shuffle controls fail to reproduce the certificate.
  - *This is last week's "bounded autonomy" thesis formalized in portfolio construction: a model proposes, a calibrated gate disposes. Directly implementable.*
- **"The End-of-Day Reversal After 2019: A Pre-Registered Test on S&P 500 Stocks Excluding Heavily Traded Names" (Nov 6, arXiv 2611.09286)** — a model *pre-registers* before computing any statistic. Result: dollar-volume-weighted reversal **3.13 bps/day (t=7.5)** in 2016–2019 and **3.05 bps (t=3.0)** in 2023–2026 — but **indistinguishable from zero in 2020–2022**, and only **1.69 bps (t=2.3)** from 2020 onward, with a one-sided 95% bound on decline of 91%. On the full index the effect falls 3.40 → 0.74 bps (registered bar of −3 not met). **No pre-registered mechanism test passes.** *A textbook demonstration of pre-registration and of why "it worked in the paper" needs a holdout. Also a live caveat: the edge lives in mid-caps, not the index.*
- **"Retrospective Analysis of Team-Following Trading Profitability in Pumpfun Meme Coins Using Funding Networks" (Nov 8, arXiv 2611.11436)** — a beautifully honest negative result. Hindsight-selected team exit thresholds: the main account ends above 1 SOL in all 8 windows with a qualifying team (1.10–11.32 SOL), but **free cash exceeds 1 SOL in only 5**, and **marks of positions that never hit their threshold make up 17–94% of equity.** Online-identifiable teams: 9 of 19 are identifiable only *after* their last launch; the remaining 10 end above 1 SOL in 10 cases, free cash above 1 SOL in 7 — but funding links still come from the full window, so it is **not a fully realisable signal.** The authors separate three different claims (large model balance, result above random cohorts that repeat the threshold search, and rejection in selection-matched nulls — 3 of 40 after Holm) and note the nulls **condition on the discovered funding graph, so they do not calibrate the whole selection procedure.** The worst window rises several-fold with a stop rule. *Read this as a template: "the difference between a backtest and an executable strategy is what survives the stop rule."*
- **"Diversification Across Predictors" (Nov 9, arXiv 2611.12389)** — bagging linear + nonlinear predictors as **specialists with low cross-covariance of errors**, with an exact bias-variance-covariance correspondence to portfolio diversification. CSI300 Alpha158: **Rank IC improvement >13% over standalone nets**, beating XGBoost and Double Ensemble. *The same diversification logic from last week's advice-layer paper, applied to the model layer.*
- **"The Logic of Factor Portfolios: Boolean Rules, Interactions, and Trading Costs" (Nov 6, arXiv 2611.09834)** — Walsh decomposition of any Boolean decision rule into single-signal and interaction exposures; **interactions are less persistent than their components, so interaction rules trade more; thresholds discard information, so at equal risk threshold implementations trade more than smooth ones.** US stocks 1973–2024: predicted-vs-realized persistence correlation ≥0.999, turnover slope 1.02. **58% of single-signal portfolios have absolute premia above quoted-spread costs (1995–2024), vs 24% of two-signal and 6% of three-signal interaction portfolios.** Out of sample, interaction portfolios raise the Sharpe of single-signal tangency portfolios. *Quantified reason why complex factor logic is usually a net negative after costs — and a warning against multi-signal confluence systems, which is exactly what an agent-built strategy tends to become.*
- **"Dynamic Scenario-Based Portfolio Optimization for Multi-Asset ETFs" (Nov 12, arXiv 2611.16540)** — ARMA–GARCH + Student-t copula scenarios vs historical moving windows, 30 US ETFs, 946 sessions: historical portfolios had higher observed reward-risk in most comparisons but **no paired difference survived multiple-testing adjustment**, and conditional scenarios did **not** consistently reduce realized downside. The dominant effect was **trading restrictions, not return modeling** — relaxing the target-adjustment limit moved exposures, turnover and returns more than scenario count or seed. *A deflationary result worth internalizing: fancy conditional scenarios did not pay, and rebalancing friction was the real variable.
